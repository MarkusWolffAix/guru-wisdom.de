# The Serpentine of History
## From the Lombards to Francis of Assisi

Human history is rarely a straight line. Rather, it resembles an interwoven path where cause and effect often span centuries and continents before their true meaning becomes fully apparent. One of the most fascinating narratives of change, adaptation, and surrender is the journey of the Lombards—a path that begins with a Germanic warrior tribe and leads us through the architectural foundations of modern finance to the spiritual teachings of Saint Francis of Assisi.

When the Lombards migrated to Italy in 568, they were a wild, wandering people, driven by raw power. But instead of simply dominating conquered Italy by the sword, they allowed themselves to be shaped by Roman and Christian culture. They abandoned their Germanic mother tongue in official documents, as evidenced by the Latin-written *Edictum Rothari* of 643. It replaced the bloody culture of revenge with a system of financial compensation. This inner willingness to relinquish one's own rigidity in order to establish structure and peace marked a gigantic leap in civilizational evolution.

From the warriors of the North emerged Europe's first great bankers, the "Lombards." They created the global "backend" of our modern financial system and effected the profound shift from physical violence to contractual trust. Instead of acquiring value through combat, they established reliable architectures such as Lombard loans, systematic recording through double-entry bookkeeping, and the system of pawnbrokers (whose symbol of the three golden balls is still present worldwide today). They built an invisible but extremely robust foundation that ordered chaos and channeled raw energy into a safe, nurturing form.

Their name traveled the world with this system: from Lombardy in northern Italy, through the financial center of London's Lombard Street, to the West Coast of America. There, in San Francisco, stands the world's most famous Lombard Street. Its extremely steep location on Russian Hill made a direct, straight route impossible in the 1920s. To make the street safe to use, eight sharp hairpin turns had to be carved into the mountain. The serpentine became an architectural necessity to prevent a precipitate fall.

This serpentine is a masterful metaphor for the journey of life itself and forms a bridge to the heart of central Italy and Saint Francis of Assisi. The Californian city bears his name, and his biography is inextricably linked to the Lombard legacy. Francis came from 12th-century Umbria, which was once under Lombard rule. His father, a cloth merchant, directly profited from the profound financial structures established by Lombard merchants. The young Francis initially sought the direct, steep path to the top: he wanted fame, worldly power, and chivalric honor. But this unchecked pace led him into a profound existential crisis.

Salvation lay in abandoning the direct route. In the marketplace of Assisi, he laid aside his fine garments, renounced the Lombard legacy of mercantile wealth, and chose absolute poverty. Francis incorporated the ultimate spiritual "serpentine" into his life: He slowed down, bowed to creation, and chose the winding, mindful path of complete surrender to the divine.

The connection of these historical threads teaches us a timeless wisdom: Whether we look at the Lombards' shift to contract-based architects, the construction of Lombard Street, or the life of Francis of Assisi—they all recognized a fundamental principle. When the terrain becomes too steep, the rigid, direct path inevitably leads to a fall. True strength and sustained growth arise when we shed our armor, slow down, and trustingly surrender to the serpentines of life.
