# The Story of Arillas and Corfu
## A Journey Through Time, Sound, and Energy

Arillas, a coastal village in northwest Corfu, has undergone a remarkable transformation over the centuries. What was once considered worthless, sandy land—a mere dowry for daughters, situated far from the safety of the mountain villages—has become an invaluable refuge.

During the Middle Ages and under Venetian rule, the sea was a source of danger. Pirate raids forced the inhabitants to retreat to higher mountain villages like Magoulades. It wasn't until the late 19th century, when the threat subsided, that the first families ventured back to the coast. They built simple huts of clay, olive wood, and pebbles and lived as self-sufficient farmers and fishermen.


A historical oddity shaped the fate of this place: Because the sandy soil on the beach was unsuitable for agriculture, this supposedly "worthless" land was given to daughters as dowries, while the fertile hills went to the sons. With the rise of tourism in the 1970s, the situation changed dramatically – the once worthless coastal plots became the most valuable lands.

In the 1980s, the locals resisted mass tourism. Instead, Swiss dropouts and Osho sannyasins discovered the place. The locals affectionately called them "gurudes." From the encounter of traditional Corfiot hospitality and the Western search for inner peace, a unique ecosystem for yoga, meditation, and alternative lifestyles emerged. The organic fusion of the self-sufficient, earth-connected way of life of the locals with the conscious, introspective quest of the first dropouts has created a unique, almost ancient sanctuary of modernity.

The origin of the name "Arillas" connects ancient languages with local history. It likely derives from a Roman officer or landowner named *Arillus*, who lived there during Roman rule. Alternatively, an origin in ancient Greek terms for certain plants or trees, such as the oak (ari-drys), is suggested.

The energetic comparison of the syllables "Ur" and "Ari" is interesting. These two syllables represent polar, yet complementary, energetic qualities. "Ur-" (The Root) points to the beginning, the source, and the end. It resonates darkly and deeply in the abdomen (similar to *Mula* or *Adi* in Sanskrit). "Ari-" (The Blossom) is an ancient Greek prefix meaning "very," "excellent," or "noble" (related to *Arya*). It strives upward, toward the light, and opens the space in the chest and head. Arillas thus embodies the energy of a noble, powerful expression – a spiritual blossom built upon the foundation of a deep, grounding history.

The island of Corfu forms the magnificent foundation for the Arillas refuge. For millennia, it has been a crossroads of Mediterranean powers. Myth tells us that Poseidon abducted the water nymph Corcyra to this island. Her descendants, the Phaeacians, were renowned in antiquity for their extraordinary hospitality. This primal energy of hospitality permeates the island to this day.

For approximately 400 years, Corfu remained under Venetian rule, protected from the Ottoman Empire. The Venetians not only shaped the architecture but also planted millions of olive trees, which continue to envelop Corfu in a constant, emerald-green mantle. Corfu has always been both: a protective shield against storms and a nurturing ground that blended influences from diverse eras into a harmonious, emerald-green essence.

Corfu, and Arillas in particular, symbolizes transformation and fusion. The island withstood storms and welcomed seekers. In Arillas, the earthy simplicity of the local people merges with the subtle aspirations of spiritual seekers. It is a vibrant synthesis of deepest roots (*Ur*) and highest bloom (*Ari*), sustained by the ancient hospitality of the Phaeacians and sheltered in the shade of centuries-old Venetian olive groves.
