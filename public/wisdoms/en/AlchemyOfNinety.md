# The Philosophy of Loving 90%
## Transformation and Focus in Everyday Life

The highest goal of human endeavor is loving thought and action. On the path to this, we inevitably encounter obstacles that must be resolved through gentle care and love. A key to this state is the conscious shift away from perfectionism toward a life-affirming philosophy of 90 percent. This attitude allows space for vitality, growth, and deep acceptance, as we transform everyday life into small, lovingly told stories.

### 1. The 90% Goal and the Illusion of Perfection
* **Loving Action:** The overarching goal is loving thought and action for humanity. Obstacles on this path are resolved with love.

* **The 90% Goal:** One is content with achieving 90% and accepts this as a fully-fledged "good." This can be achieved in a healthy and fulfilling way.

* **The 100% Trap:** The last 10% to supposed perfection are the most difficult and time-consuming.

* **Theoretical Boundaries:** The boundaries of the evaluation ("very good" / 1 and "unsatisfactory" / 6) exist only theoretically and should generally not be reached.

* **Reason:** Perfection is always based solely on the *current* state of knowledge. If you think something through to its "perfect" conclusion, there is a risk that you will not revisit it or develop it further later.

### 2. Empathetic and Non-Comparable Evaluation

* Evaluations should be made exclusively with love and empathy.

* A "good" is always a "good" for the *respective feeling being*.

* This "good" should not be compared to the "good" of another being – this is a fundamental prerequisite for self-love and self-acceptance.


* * One is dedicated and satisfied with one's own performance, but learns from others who seem to be doing the same thing, and vice versa (without judgment).

### 3. Loving Processes and the Focus on Development

* Processes of love can simply coexist if they share the same abstract goal. They are not judged against each other.

* **Process over Result:** The primary focus is not on the bare result. The abstract goal is secondary.

* **Solidarity:** What's important is helping each other and approaching one another with empathy.

* **The Absolute Focus is Love:** Not the result itself, but the beauty of the result and the beauty within the development of the goal—supported by empathy in the process—is the true goal.

### 4. Resolving the Flaw Block (Focus Correction)

* **The Problem:** We deliver a good result (e.g., a presentation or a picture) that we mostly like, but we fixate on the one tiny point that we *don't* like.

* **The Rule:** This must not happen – neither in self-criticism nor in criticism of others! Positive aspects must always dominate our (self-)evaluation and resonate much more strongly than the details that need improvement.

* **Feelings as a Guidepost:** The goal is for us to feel good, not bad, at all times. No negative thoughts about ourselves or others.

* **Divine Action:** Since God is love, this loving help and feeling better corresponds to divine action. If we resolved these blockages worldwide, there would be fewer or even no problems.

### 5. The 90/10 Rule of Sensory Impressions in Everyday Life

* **Soak Up 90% Beauty:** One should consciously try to absorb at least 90% beautiful sensory impressions (beautiful images, beautiful sounds) every day so that one feels profoundly good at the end of the day. This is extremely important.


* **10% Transformation Work:** The remaining 10% of the day are not annoyances, but conscious "tasks." Their function is to actively transform negative images and impressions into positive ones.
