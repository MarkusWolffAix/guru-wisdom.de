✨The Kiss of Forgetting
## The Gift of Not Knowing

Before a child sees the light of day, ancient legends tell us, its soul rests in a place of absolute knowledge. In this heavenly stillness, the unborn know every secret of the universe. They understand the movement of the stars, the whisper of the wind, and the true meaning of life. But this all-encompassing knowledge is too heavy and too immense for earthly existence.

When the first days after birth arrive and the newborn slumbers wearily in its crib, something magical happens. The room fills with a soft, barely perceptible light, and an angel silently approaches the crib. It is the Angel of Forgetting—often associated with the angel Layla in ancient Jewish traditions—whose task is as wistful as it is loving.

The angel leans over the peacefully breathing child. He gazes at the tiny face, still radiating the mirror-like clarity of eternity in its sleep. With a tender gesture, the celestial being raises its hand. Gently, the angel places its index finger on the small hollow between the baby's nose and upper lip and whispers a soft, barely audible, "Shh..."

At that precise moment, cosmic knowledge fades from the child's memory. All that remains is a physical mark of this heavenly touch: the philtrum, that small indentation above the upper lip. The memories of eternity fade into a distant, intangible dream. The newborn forgets everything it knew—not as a punishment, but as a perfect gift.

Why does this myth continue to fascinate us today? From a purely biological and developmental psychology perspective, we are, of course, not born with the entire encyclopedic knowledge of the cosmos. We are born with instincts, evolutionary heritage, and a brain that, like an empty yet highly complex net, is ready to absorb the world. But on a metaphorical and spiritual level, this story captures a deeper truth: the innocence of not knowing is the very prerequisite for earthly life itself.

If we already knew every answer, every outcome, and the entire meaning of the universe, there would be no surprise and no genuine wonder. There would be no friction from which we could learn, and no mistakes to teach us compassion and forgiveness. Forgetting is so beautifully understood in this text as grace. It creates the space in which free will, curiosity, genuine human connection, and unpredictable love can even begin to flourish. Only through this forgetting can a person rediscover the world with wide, wonder-filled eyes. Only in this way can we learn to speak, to err, to fall in love, and to experience life in all its painful and beautiful facets.

Before your first breath,
knowledge lay still in your hand.

You knew every star's journey,

and learn to marvel, bit by bit, from today onward."
