# I am a mother to this world
## How the unassuming Sanskrit syllable "Ma" reveals the path to universal love and spiritual creative power

This reflection documents a profound philosophical journey: Starting with the simple question of a Sanskrit word, a powerful spiritual life task crystallized. At its core lies the realization that the syllable "Ma" is far more than a grammatical pronoun – it is a hidden clue and call to become a nurturing, guiding force in this world.

Here is a detailed summary of these discoveries, focusing on the central message of universal motherhood:

### 1. The hidden seed in the syllable "Ma"

If we consider the purely linguistic level first, the word *mā* in Sanskrit has two everyday meanings: It functions as a gentle, wishful "not" (a prohibition) or as the unstressed pronoun "me". But behind this simple facade lies a far deeper resonance.

* **The creative power:** The etymological root of the syllable *mā* means “to measure, form, create”.
* **The origin of the mother:** The actual Sanskrit word for mother – *Mātṛ* (or *Mātā* ) – is derived precisely from this root. The mother is that force which “forms” life from the formless and makes the world comprehensible to the child.
* **The sound (Nada):** In Indian thought, grammar is often secondary to the energetic vibration of a word. When one pronounces or sings "Ma," the acoustic essence of the universal mother and her creative power inevitably resonates. It is a deep, unconscious remembrance of this primal energy.

### 2. The properties of a universal nut

The bridge from the pure syllable to the lived purpose of life is formed by an intuitive insight: The supplications, often linked to Indian mantras (such as the call from the untrue to the true and from darkness to light), describe, at their core, profoundly maternal qualities. The "Ma" is thus a direct indication not only to seek these qualities externally, but to embody them oneself.

* **Leadership and protection:** To live up to a maternal role for the world means adopting an attitude of unconditional love. It is about holding space and taking people by the hand like a mother, leading them from darkness (ignorance, suffering, ego) into light (knowledge, peace, truth).
* **Spiritual motherhood (Lokamata):** This role is completely separate from biological motherhood. In Sanskrit, there is the established, venerable term *Lokamata* (Mother of the World) or *Jaganmata* (Universal Mother) for this. Holy women in India are often simply called "Ma" because they embody precisely this protective, all-encompassing love.

### 3. From Receiving to Giving: The Path of “Ma”

How does one become this mother to the world? It is a cyclical process deeply rooted in the ancient texts of India – such as Shaktism or the veneration of the *Veda Mata* Gayatri.

* **The act of asking (receiving):** Initially, the "Ma" ("me") expresses the humility to be guided. One opens oneself to receiving the light and the truth within.
* **The Transformation:** The Divine Mother (Devi) is described in the scriptures as the "lamp that dispels darkness." If one allows oneself to be nourished by this energy, one inevitably becomes this lamp oneself.
* **The act of radiating (giving):** The former "me" ( *Lead me* ) transforms. When one's own vessel is filled with this light, the "Ma" becomes a mission: One becomes a mother ( *Mātṛ* ) who passes on this healing, ordering, and loving energy to one's environment, profession, or fellow human beings.

### 4. The visual metaphor: The image of the Lokamata

To complete this intellectual journey, the image of the *Lokamata* serves as an archetypal embodiment of our understanding. It captures the visual language of ancient Indian philosophy and makes the meaning of the syllable "Ma" tangible.

* **The limitless capacity:** The depiction with multiple arms symbolizes the ability to nurture, protect, and lead simultaneously. 
* **The radiant primordial sound:** Above the figure shines the Sanskrit symbol **माँ (Mā)** as the energetic source from which all maternal creative power springs.
* **The symbols of care:** In her hands she carries the signs of her task: The lotus for spiritual purity, the seed for nourishing the earth, the child for unconditional love and the globe for universal responsibility.
* **The shared spark:** The people who turn to her reflect the process of receiving. Through her presence, they themselves are encouraged to carry the light further and become small "lamps" of truth in their own lives.

**The core insight:** The matriarchal essence of Indian spirituality is revealed here in its fullest bloom. The syllable "Ma" is not simply a word; it is a constant reminder of one's own immeasurable potential. It is the subtle indication that the ultimate task is not merely to seek salvation or guidance, but to become a *Lokamata* —a loving, nurturing mother for this world.
