# Amavasya
## The Abundance of Darkness

In many modern cultures, darkness is often avoided, associated with emptiness or even fear. But if we turn our gaze eastward and delve deep into the traditions of Hinduism, we encounter *Amavasya* – the ancient Sanskrit word for the new moon night. Literally translated, it means something like "dwelling together" or "dwelling together." It describes that cosmic moment when the sun and moon are united in the sky, and the moon becomes completely invisible to our eyes.

Amavasya teaches us a fundamental and profoundly healing perspective: This deepest darkness of the sky is not empty, but absolutely full. It is like the dark, fertile soil in which a seed rests before it germinates. In this apparent emptiness lies the entire potential of creation.

Spiritually speaking, this night is a cosmic moment of respite. It is a natural pause in the endless cycle of becoming and passing away. Traditionally, this time is used to pause and turn inward. It is the ideal phase to empty the mind, shed old, unhelpful habits, and let go. Amavasya is also the time to honor one's ancestors—a return to the roots in the darkness of the past that give us support.

We all experience moments of darkness in life, or phases in which seemingly nothing happens. Amavasya reminds us not to fear these phases, but to recognize them for what they are: the necessary, fertile period of rest in which the old can rest to make room for a completely new cycle of light.
