# The Path to Adulthood
## Responsibility, Inner Reconciliation, and Caring for Oneself and Others

Growing up is not a fixed state reached at a specific age, but rather a lifelong, inner process of maturation. At the beginning of our journey, we often believe that being an adult means functioning flawlessly, knowing all the answers, and maintaining a rigid outward facade. However, over time, this understanding fundamentally changes. We realize that no one possesses a perfect master plan and that true adulthood lies in the ability to take full responsibility for one's actions despite all uncertainties.

A key milestone on this path is reconciliation with one's own biography. Childhood shapes us profoundly, and we often carry wounds, burdens, or unfulfilled longings into our adult lives. Becoming an adult therefore also means accepting one's own childhood—with all its light and shadow aspects. Bringing all the pain and burdens left behind into the light and processing them, often with the necessary external support, is an act of courage. It frees us from the unconscious repetition of old patterns and grants us the freedom to reshape our lives through our own strength.

Inextricably linked to this is the art of genuine self-care. Those who have learned to care for the wounded parts within themselves cease to seek validation or salvation externally. This turning inward is by no means selfish; rather, it is the fundamental prerequisite for being truly, empathetically, and powerfully present for others. Only when we have learned to take good care of ourselves do we possess the emotional maturity to assume responsibility for the well-being of others and to collaboratively create a connected, conscious world.
