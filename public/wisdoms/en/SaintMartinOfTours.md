# The Legend of Saint Martin
## A Life of Charity and Compassion

Martin of Tours was born in 316 AD and joined the Roman army as a young man. The most famous legend about him, still celebrated today, took place on a bitterly cold winter day in the French city of Amiens.

**The Encounter at the City Gate** As the soldier Martin rode his horse toward the city gate of Amiens, a poor, half-naked beggar sat there in the snow. The man was shivering miserably and begged passersby for help, but everyone ignored him. Martin had neither money nor food with him, but he wore the thick, warm cloak of the imperial cavalry. He drew his sword, cut his cloak in two, and gave one half to the freezing beggar. The onlookers laughed at him because he now looked ridiculous with only half a cloak, but Martin didn't care.

**The Dream**
The following night, Jesus Christ appeared to Martin in a dream. He was wearing the very half of the cloak that Martin had given to the beggar. Jesus spoke to the angels surrounding him: "Martin, who is not yet baptized, has clothed me with this cloak." This experience changed Martin's life. He left the army, was baptized, and from then on dedicated his life to the poor and the Christian faith.

**The Legend of St. Martin's Geese**
Martin lived very modestly. Because he was so kind and helpful, the citizens of Tours wanted to make him their bishop years later. However, Martin did not feel worthy of this high office. To avoid being elected, he hid in a goose pen. But the geese cackled so loudly at the unexpected visitor that the searching citizens found him. Thus, in the year 371, Martin was finally consecrated Bishop of Tours.

Many traditions still commemorate this event today, celebrated every year on St. Martin's Day (November 11th). November** (his funeral day):

* **Lantern Parades:** Children walk through the streets with glowing lanterns to carry on the light that Martin brought into the world.

* **St. Martin's Goose:** The traditional goose dinner is a reminder of the cackling birds that betrayed him.

* **St. Martin's Pastries:** Regional baked goods such as the Weckmann (or Stutenkerl) and the Martin's pretzel are often shared symbolically.
