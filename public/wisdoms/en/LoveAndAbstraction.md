# Love and abstraction
## The mind as servant of the heart

### Summary of the philosophical reflection
Love is often viewed as a pure, instinctive force that seeks common ground, forgives, and strives for a deep fusion. But this view falls short if it excludes reason and abstraction as the counterparts of emotion. In truth, love undergoes a mature, philosophical cycle in which abstraction—the conscious, often cognitive, "zooming out"—plays a central role. 

As soon as love abstracts, it creates a temporary distance. This distance, however, is not an end in itself and is by no means synonymous with emotional coldness or rejection. Rather, it serves the purpose of understanding. One takes a step back to grasp patterns, logics, or conflicts. Once this process is complete, what has been learned is brought back into love. The abstracted is lovingly and comprehensibly translated, allowing love to better understand itself and grow.

The tools to build this bridge between knowledge and feeling are empathy and a change of perspective:
* **Empathy** is the heart's tool. It operates with emotional immediacy and warmth, bypassing logic. It allows us to feel what the other person feels.
* **Changing perspectives** is the tool of the mind. It is the cognitive bridge that allows us to leave our own point of view and see the world through the eyes of a loved one.

The crucial core idea of this reflection is this: **The mind is an abstraction of the heart.** The heart sends the mind on a journey of discovery to grasp things that might be too complex for mere feeling. The mind, in itself, has no end in itself and must not lose itself in pure analysis. Its true purpose is to continually bring the acquired knowledge back to the heart in order to nurture and enrich love and deepen understanding.
