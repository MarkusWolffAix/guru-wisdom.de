# Aachen Cathedral
## Built Theology and Cosmic Order

Aachen Cathedral is far more than just stacked stone; it is a gigantic repository of centuries-old prayers, earthly claims to power, and profound spiritual longing. Conceived by Charlemagne in the late 8th century, it was not designed as an ordinary building. It is, in the truest sense of the word **, "theology made manifest"** —the architectural attempt to bring a biblical vision, the *Heavenly Jerusalem* , physically to earth.

The core of this vision is the so-called **Carolingian** central building. This term derives from the Latin name *Carolus* (Charles) and refers to the Carolingian dynasty. The 8th and 9th centuries, a period of immense cultural and educational renewal, are known as the Carolingian Renaissance. Architecturally, "Carolingian" represents the ambitious attempt to fuse ancient Roman monumentality with profound Christian symbolism. This masterpiece unites sacred geometry, mysticism, and historical roots into a resonant field that continues to touch people today.

### The Octagon, the Living Stones, and the Measure of Revelation

The heart of the cathedral is the famous Octagon, the original Palatine Chapel. The architectural choice of the number eight is no accident, but highly symbolic: the number eight represents the resurrection and the "eighth day" of creation – the beginning of eternity. At the same time, the octagon mathematically mediates between the square (symbolizing the earth) and the circle (symbolizing heaven). Anyone standing inside the Octagon is symbolically at the precise intersection between the transient world and the eternal kingdom of God.

This striving for perfect divine order is also manifest in the invisible dimensions. The floor plan and proportions are based on a system of measurement that directly quotes the Book of Revelation (chapter 21). In the Bible, the city wall of the New Jerusalem is described as having a height of 144 cubits. Charlemagne used the Carolingian foot (approximately 33.27 cm) as the basic unit – and thus the height of the octagon from the ground to the apex of the dome measures exactly 144 Carolingian feet. The number 144 (12 x 12) represents the perfected divine order, based on the 12 tribes of Israel and the 12 apostles.

This harmony is echoed in Alcuin's original Latin inscription on the building. It speaks of the "living stones" ( *lapides vivi* ), held together by the bond of peace, and of the "harmonious numbers" ( *pares numeros* ). The inscription simultaneously honors God as the cosmic architect and Emperor Charlemagne as the secular founder. 

But where humankind strives for the highest light, fascinating shadows often take root. The legend of the Wolf's Door tells of a pact with the devil, made by the builders in their financial straits. They outwitted him by driving a wolf into the cathedral as the first soul. The deceived devil, rushing out, slammed the bronze door so hard that his thumb became caught in the doorknob – a tangible detail of ancient myth-making.

### The heavenly Jerusalem of light and matter

Charlemagne built his Church of St. Mary as an exact replica of the city of God, which is reflected not only in its dimensions but also in the materials used. The use of antique marble, heavy, gleaming bronze, and thousands of gold mosaics was intended to make the biblical description of a city of "pure gold like crystal-clear glass" visually tangible.

As soon as the sun strikes the mosaics in the vaulted ceiling, the walls seem to shimmer. In this vast cosmic mandala, Christ is enthroned as ruler of the world (Pantocrator), surrounded by the 24 Elders of the Apocalypse, who offer him their crowns in an act of utmost humility. The slightly angled gold and glass tiles capture the light and sacred sounds in the room, so that the mosaic seems to breathe on the sound waves of human voices.

Directly beneath this protective sky hangs the Barbarossa Chandelier (12th century). This enormous wheel chandelier, commissioned by Emperor Frederick Barbarossa, depicts the descent of the divine to earth. It is a direct architectural model of the city wall of the Heavenly Jerusalem, crowned by exactly 12 towers. The ornate inscription explicitly names it *Celica Jherusalem* .

### A center of devotion and history

For over 1200 years, the cathedral has been an uninterrupted center of human devotion. Every seven years, the pilgrimage draws tens of thousands of pilgrims to the Shrine of Our Lady. 

While the octagon represents eternity, other parts of the cathedral bear witness to its vibrant history. A fascinating example of this is the **Hungarian Chapel** . It was founded in the 14th century to provide the countless Hungarian pilgrims who flocked to the pilgrimage site with a dedicated space for services in their native language. The current chapel is an 18th-century Baroque reconstruction designed by the architect Joseph Moretti. This striking architectural style forms a compelling visual contrast to the Gothic choir and the austere Carolingian octagon, demonstrating how organically the cathedral has evolved over the ages.

In contrast to the vibrant pilgrimage scene, the simple marble throne in the upper ambulatory stands out. It is said to be made from slabs of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem and served for centuries as the coronation seat for German kings. This almost austere contrast to the otherwise magnificent splendor unmistakably symbolizes that even the highest secular power must ultimately submit to the cosmic order.

### The polarity of window and floor

As a whole, the cathedral reveals a tremendous architectural and spiritual tension between above and below:

* **The Windows (The Dissolution of Matter):** In the Gothic choir, the so-called "glass house," gigantic glass surfaces replace the massive walls. Light, as the most direct manifestation of the divine, streams in. The dissolution of solid matter represents omnipresent grace and the permeability of earthly boundaries.
* **The floor (the foundation):** The floor, made of antique columns and marble slabs that Charlemagne had transported from Rome and Ravenna, forms the historical anchor. It symbolizes solid matter, the earthly root, and gravity, without which no spiritual aspiration can rise.

Precisely in between, in the empty space, stands humankind. We are the "living stones," the connecting line between the ancient, solid marble of the earth and the weightless, descending light of the sky – eternally destined to maintain the balance between deep rootedness and spiritual expansion.

### The Aachen bust of Charlemagne: Reliquary and symbol of power
The bust, commissioned around **1349** by Emperor Charles IV, is one of the most important works of Gothic art.

* **Reliquary:** It contains the **original skullcap of Charlemagne** . Anthropological studies (2014) confirm the authenticity of the remains.
* **Heraldic symbolism:** The robe displays the **imperial eagle** (Holy Roman Empire) and the fleur-de- **lis** (for France). This marks Charlemagne as the common origin of both nations.
* **Details:** The bust wears a removable Gothic fleur-de-lis crown, adorned with ancient Roman **cameos** to emphasize its continuity with the Roman Empire.
* **Realism:** Despite idealized features (full beard instead of the historically probable mustache), it is considered an early milestone in individual portrait art.

### Aachen as coronation site (936–1531)
Between the coronations of Otto I and Ferdinand I, Aachen was the rightful place for the investiture of the Holy Roman Emperors. Crucially, they were enthroned on the **throne of Charlemagne** . The privilege of coronation traditionally rested with the **Archbishop of Cologne** .

### End of the Aachen era
From **1562** (Maximilian II) onwards, the kings were crowned in **Frankfurt am Main** . The reasons were purely practical: elections took place in Frankfurt, the city was geographically more central, and the logistical advantages outweighed the ritual connection to Charlemagne's palace.

---

### International dimensions of the empire
Although the empire later acquired the suffix "of the German Nation," it was a supranational entity. Many rulers were culturally international (e.g., the **Luxembourgs** in Prague or **Charles V** in Flanders and Spain). This underscores the European core of the empire, which originated in the person of Charlemagne.
