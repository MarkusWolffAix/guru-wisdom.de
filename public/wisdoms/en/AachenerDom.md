# Aachen Cathedral
## Built Theology and Cosmic Order

Aachen Cathedral is far more than just stacked stone; it is a gigantic repository of centuries-old prayers, earthly claims to power, and profound spiritual longing. Conceived by Charlemagne in the late 8th century, it was not designed as an ordinary building. It is, in the truest sense of the word, **"Theology Built"** – the architectural attempt to physically bring a biblical vision, the *Heavenly Jerusalem*, to earth.

The core of this vision is the so-called **Carolingian** central building. This term derives from the Latin name *Carolus* (Charles) and refers to the Carolingian dynasty. The era of the 8th and 9th centuries, in which immense cultural and educational renewal took place, is known as the Carolingian Renaissance. Architecturally, "Carolingian" represents the ambitious attempt to fuse ancient Roman monumentality with profound Christian symbolism. This masterpiece unites sacred geometry, mysticism, and historical roots into a resonant space that continues to touch people today.

### The Octagon, the Living Stones, and the Measure of Revelation

The heart of the cathedral is the famous Octagon, the original Palatine Chapel. The architectural choice of the number eight is no accident, but highly symbolic: The number eight represents the resurrection and the "eighth day" of creation—the beginning of eternity. At the same time, the octagon mathematically mediates between the square (symbolizing the earth) and the circle (symbolizing heaven). Anyone standing within the Octagon is symbolically at the precise intersection between the transient world and the eternal kingdom of God.

This striving for perfect divine order also manifests itself in the invisible dimensions. The floor plan and proportions are based on a system of measurement that directly references the Book of Revelation (chapter 21). The Bible describes the city wall of the New Jerusalem as having a height of 144 cubits. Charlemagne used the Carolingian foot (approximately 33.27 cm) as his base unit – and thus the height of the octagon from the ground to the apex of the dome measures exactly 144 Carolingian feet. The number 144 (12 x 12) represents the perfect divine order, based on the 12 tribes of Israel and the 12 apostles.

This harmony is echoed in the original Latin inscription by Alcuin. It speaks of the "living stones" (*lapides vivi*), held together by the bond of peace, and of the "harmonious numbers" (*pares numeros*). The inscription simultaneously honors God as the cosmic architect and Emperor Charlemagne as the secular founder.

But where humankind strives for the highest light, fascinating shadows often take root. The legend of the Wolf's Door tells of a pact with the devil, made by the builders in their financial straits. They outwitted him by driving a wolf into the cathedral as the first soul. The deceived devil, rushing out, slammed the bronze door so hard that his thumb caught in the doorknob – a tangible detail of ancient myth-making.

### The Heavenly Jerusalem of Light and Matter

Charles built his St. Mary's Church as an exact replica of the city of God, which is reflected not only in its dimensions but also in the materials. The use of antique marble, heavy, gleaming bronze, and thousands upon thousands of gold mosaics was intended to make the biblical description of a city of "pure gold like crystal-clear glass" visually tangible.

As soon as the sun strikes the mosaics in the vaulted ceiling, the walls seem to shimmer. In this vast cosmic mandala, Christ Pantocrator, ruler of the world, is enthroned, surrounded by the 24 Elders of the Apocalypse, who offer him their crowns in an act of profound humility. The slightly angled gold and glass tiles capture the light and sacred sound in the space, so that the mosaic seems to breathe on the sound waves of human voices.

Directly beneath this protective canopy hangs the Barbarossa Chandelier (12th century). This colossal wheel chandelier, commissioned by Emperor Frederick Barbarossa, depicts the descent of the divine to earth. It is a direct architectural model of the city wall of the Heavenly Jerusalem, crowned by exactly 12 towers. The ornate inscription explicitly names it *Celica Jherusalem*.

### A Center of Devotion and History

For over 1200 years, the Cathedral has been an unbroken center of human devotion. Every seven years, the pilgrimage draws tens of thousands of pilgrims to the Shrine of Our Lady.

While the octagon represents eternity, other parts of the cathedral bear witness to its vibrant history. A fascinating example of this is the Hungarian Chapel. It was founded in the 14th century to provide the countless Hungarian pilgrims who flocked to the pilgrimage with a dedicated space for worship in their native language. The current chapel is an 18th-century Baroque structure designed by the architect Joseph Moretti. This striking architectural style forms a compelling visual contrast to the Gothic choir and the austere Carolingian octagon, demonstrating how organically the cathedral has evolved over the ages.

In contrast to the vibrant pilgrimage scene, the simple marble throne in the upper ambulatory stands out. It is said to be made from slabs of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem and served for centuries as the coronation seat for German kings. This almost austere contrast to the otherwise magnificent splendor unmistakably symbolizes that even the highest earthly power must ultimately submit to the cosmic order.

### The Polarity of Window and Floor

In its entirety, the cathedral reveals a tremendous architectural and spiritual tension between above and below:

* **The Windows (The Dissolution of Matter):** In the Gothic choir, the so-called "glass house," gigantic expanses of glass replace the massive walls. Light, as the most direct manifestation of the divine, streams in. The dissolution of solid matter represents omnipresent grace and the permeability of earthly boundaries.

* **The Floor (The Foundation):** The floor, made of ancient columns and marble slabs that Charlemagne had transported from Rome and Ravenna, forms the historical anchor. It symbolizes solid matter, the earthly root, and gravity, without which no spiritual striving can reach for the heavens.

Precisely between these two elements, in the empty space, stands humankind. We are the “living stones,” the connecting line between the ancient, solid marble of the earth and the weightless, descending light of the heavens—eternally destined to maintain the balance between deep roots and spiritual expansion.

### The Aachen Bust of Charlemagne: Reliquary and Symbol of Power
The bust, commissioned by Emperor Charles IV around 1349, is one of the most important works of Gothic art.

* **Reliquary:** It contains the **original skullcap of Charlemagne**. Anthropological studies (2014) confirm the authenticity of the remains.

* **Heraldic Symbolism:** The robe displays the **Imperial Eagle** (Holy Roman Empire) and the **Lily** (Fleur-de-Lys for France). This marks Charlemagne as the common origin of both nations.


* * **Details:** The bust wears a detachable Gothic fleur-de-lis crown adorned with antique Roman cameos to emphasize its continuity with the Roman Empire.

* **Realism:** Despite idealized features (a full beard instead of the historically probable mustache), it is considered an early milestone in individual portraiture.

### Aachen as a Coronation Site (936–1531)
Between the coronations of Otto I and Ferdinand I, Aachen was the rightful location for the investiture of the Holy Roman Emperors. Crucially, they were enthroned on the throne of Charlemagne. The privilege of coronation traditionally rested with the Archbishop of Cologne.

### End of the Aachen Era

From **1562** (Maximilian II) onward, the kings were crowned in **Frankfurt am Main**. The reasons were purely practical: Elections took place in Frankfurt, the city was geographically more central, and the logistical advantages outweighed the ritual connection to Charlemagne's palace.

### International Dimensions of the Empire
Although the empire later received the suffix "of the German Nation," it was a supranational entity. Many rulers had an international cultural background (e.g., the **Luxembourgers** in Prague or **Charles V** in Flanders and Spain). This underscores the European core of the empire, which originated in the person of Charlemagne.
