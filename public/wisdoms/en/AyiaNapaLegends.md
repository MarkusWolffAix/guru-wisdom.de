# Ayia Napa
## History and legends of a holiday paradise

### 🏖️ From forest area to holiday paradise
Ayia Napa in Cyprus is now world-famous for its stunning beaches (such as Nissi Beach) and vibrant nightlife. Originally, however, the region was a dense, uninhabited forest. It wasn't until the mid-18th century that refugees settled there. After the Turkish invasion of Famagusta, the island's then-popular tourist center, in 1974, the small fishing village of Ayia Napa rapidly developed into the island's new tourist hotspot.

### 🏰 The Monastery and the Two Legends
The heart and historical origin of the place is the monastery dating back to the Venetian period (around 1500 AD). Its origin is based on two mystical legends:
1. **The Hunter and the Light:** A hunter, thanks to his dog, found a luminous icon of the Virgin Mary in a cave deep in the forest. It was likely hidden there during the Byzantine Iconoclasm in the 8th century to protect it from destruction. The icon became known as *Panagia Napa* ("Holy Virgin of the Woods") and gave the place its name.
2. **The Venetian noblewoman:** A wealthy aristocrat from nearby Famagusta fell in love with a commoner. When her father forbade the marriage, she fled to that very cave, dedicated her life to God, and financed the construction of the fortified monastery. She also planted a sycamore tree at the south gate, which still stands in the courtyard today and is over 600 years old.

To bring these stories to life, we created an image of the monastery courtyard depicting the noblewoman planting the tree and the hunter discovering the cave. This inspired the atmospheric neo-folk song **"The Octagonal Pulse,"** which musically recounts these legends.
