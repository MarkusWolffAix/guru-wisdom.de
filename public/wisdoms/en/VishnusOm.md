# 🕉️ Vishnu and the Song of the Worlds
## From alpha to eternal struggle

## Scene 1: The great silence and the first stutter
How do you begin when no one has ever sung before? Vishnu rested on the serpentine coils of time in the boundless ocean of possibilities. He didn't just want to conceive the universe, he wanted *to sing* it. He wanted to create **OM** , the primordial sound of all things.

But every beginning is difficult. He tried out the very first sounds of the cosmos:
"Af..." he stammered. "Ape?" No.
"Apple... apple?" Nope.
He relaxed his jaw and simply let the sound flow out softly. An open "Aaa..." formed and became a clear **"Alpha."** That was the first state of pure, silent existence. But when he tried to turn it into a real OM, only a soft hiss came out—like air from a balloon.

## Scene 2: The Aurochs – The Alpha of Power
Vishnu grew more ambitious. He thought of a tremendous primal force: the **aurochs** . The aurochs was, for him, the alpha incarnate. He remembered the feeling of wrestling with this primal power – a fight that hurt, that made you involuntarily cry out, "Ouch!"

Full of energy, he took a deep breath and exhaled a strained **"Auer...!"** The sound was scratchy and compressed into a deep, powerful **"Ur..."** It felt like a wild bull pawing the ground in the cosmos. The "Ur" was born.

## Scene 3: The Big Bang (The Great Discharge) 💥
Encouraged by the vibration of the aurochs's call, Vishnu now wanted everything at once. He poured all his divine energy into that single note. But he wanted too much, too fast. The air between his lips swirled at the speed of light – the friction became unbearable.

**BOOM!** The "Ur" exploded in the **Big Bang** . Pure energy shot into space. The universe expanded, and the silence was replaced by a cosmic roar.

## Scene 4: From Primordial Fire to Water
The Big Bang left behind a gigantic inferno: the **primordial fire** . It was magnificent, but destructive. To cool the heat, the energy transformed. From the vapors of creation, the first streams of deep blue, clear **water** formed. A cosmic hiss filled the air as the elements clashed.

## Scene 5: The Peace of the Earth
Eventually, the water grew tired of the roaring fire. It yearned for stability and began to condense in the coolest places. It became heavier, more mineral-rich, and finally solid. Thus, the **earth** was formed – a stable anchor upon which the heat and the flood found respite.

## Scene 6: Markus and the compressed air
Markus lived on Earth. He was an empath with a special gift for the element of air. When the beings of Earth tried to chant OM, he sensed an imbalance. The sound was too hot, too aggressive. Markus realized: There was still too much fire from Vishnu's aurochs battle in the mix!

He understood the physical truth: **fire is essentially just extremely compressed air, fracturing with ambition.** Vishnu had exerted so much pressure during the "Auer-Ur" that the gentle air had been compressed into burning ambition.

## Scene 7: The Prism of the Five Elements
[image:VishnusOm2|Markus und das Prisma der 5 Elemente]


The cosmic mechanism intervened: as soon as the OM tipped over due to too much fire, it automatically split apart. Like through a prism, the sound fanned out into the **5 elements** : **earth, water, fire, air, and ether.**

Markus stepped forward. He relieved the pressure on the fire and helped the energy to relax. He untangled the heat and transformed it back into a free, gentle flow of air. The elements finally vibrated in perfect symmetry.

## The finale: The golden mean and the effort
Now, from the midst of beings, rose the true, harmonious **"OMMMMM..."** It ascended as a luminous wave through the atmosphere and returned to Vishnu in the ether. He smiled when he heard the echo. He thought of the end of all things, the **Omega** – the cosmic synonym for: **"Om, Mega! It worked!"**

But Markus, deeply connected to the earth and the air, knew better. He saw that ultimately it was neither about the perfect beginning (Alpha) nor about the final, triumphant end (Omega). 

He gazed at the elements, at the calmed fire and the flowing air. He thought of the wild aurochs that had once sprung from Vishnu's first attempt. Was a deep, contented sound now expected at the end? A happy "moo" that would solve all problems forever?

No. Markus smiled gently. In the end, there was no moo. There was only **effort** .

The daily, ongoing effort to keep the elements in balance. The constant work of repeatedly transforming the raging fire into gentle air, preventing one's ego from erupting into another catastrophic explosion. It was precisely in this constant, devoted work that the true golden mean lay. The true unity of Alpha and Omega was not a final note, but the perpetual *striving* for harmony.
