# Our Cosmic Home
## A Journey from Earth to the Infinity of the Galactic Web

When we gaze at the starry sky on a clear night, we see more than just distant lights. We gaze upon the architecture of our own origin, upon a vast home that envelops us on countless levels. This cosmic home is not a static space, but a living, pulsating organism of matter, light, and gravity.

Our journey begins on a small scale: our solar system. Formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago, our Sun and its planets are relative latecomers in the history of the universe. We owe our existence precisely to this late appearance. Only through the death of earlier, colossal generations of stars were those heavy elements forged and hurled into space from which our Earth, and ultimately we ourselves, are made. We rest in a tiny corner, in the Local Flake of our spiral arm—the Orion Arm.


We are part of the Milky Way, a majestic barred spiral galaxy. With its hundreds of billions of stars and a diameter of over 100,000 light-years, it is an immeasurable ocean of luminous gas, dark dust, and ancient systems. And yet, it is not immortal, but in a constant state of transformation. It grows, absorbs smaller companions, and is inexorably moving toward its greatest metamorphosis: In the distant future, it will merge with the Andromeda Galaxy and take on a completely new form – Milkomeda.

But the Milky Way is just one neighborhood in a much larger city. If we zoom out further, we see immense currents. Our galaxy is being pulled toward the Laniakea Supercluster by unimaginable gravitational forces, such as the Great Attractor. We are swimming in a cosmic river, together with hundreds of thousands of other galaxies.

On the grandest scale of all, the cosmic web is finally revealed. The universe resembles a luminous, sponge-like structure. Infinite threads of dark matter and galaxies traverse space, intertwined in the luminous nodes of vast superclusters, separated by gigantic, eerily silent voids.

In this unfathomable infinity, we may appear vanishingly small, like a speck of dust in the wind. Yet this awareness inspires not terror, but profound humility and interconnectedness. We are not separate from this immense creation; we are made of its very fabric. Within us, in our minds and our capacity to gaze at the stars, the universe has found a way to contemplate itself and its infinite home.
