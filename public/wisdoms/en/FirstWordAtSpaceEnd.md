# The first word at the end of the room
## When the ending of space and room touches the origin

In the contemplation of language and sound, a geometry sometimes emerges that goes far beyond mere wordplay. Looking at the words "Raum" ( **space)** and **"Room** ," one initially observes an outward movement: the initial **"R"** stands at the beginning of both words because in Sanskrit, every consonant already contains the first breath of the vowel – thus, the **"R"** becomes the vibrant, fiery sound **"Ra"** (the root of the bija mantra *RAM* and sonically related to the creative sunlight *Ra* ), which provides the impetus and opens up the vastness. But if one follows the word to its **end** , precisely what is considered the very first beginning in sound mysticism emerges: **AUM** and **OM** .

The end of space is the first word. In this single sentence, a vast, mystical arc of creation is completed. In the yogic tradition, the primordial sound AUM is revered as *Pranava* , the first sound from which the universe unfolds ( *Nada Brahma* – "The world is sound"). In other wisdom traditions as well, the word, the creative sound that existed before all form, time, and physical space, stands at the beginning of all things.

The fact that the very sounds of the words *"Raum" (space)* and " *Room"* culminate in this first word fundamentally transforms our understanding of boundaries and ends. A space doesn't end at a rigid wall, nor does it dissolve into nothingness. Rather, traversing a space to its outermost boundary inevitably leads back to its own origin. End and beginning are not separate points on a straight line, but touch each other like the beginning and end of a circle.

This movement can be understood as the great breath of creation. The first word—the undivided **AUM/OM** —breathes out the world, gives it extension, experience, and form through the impulse of the **R** , and brings **space** ( *Akasha* ) into being. But as soon as this space is completely traversed and we reach its end, the separation dissolves again, and nothing remains but that first sound itself, from which everything originated.

Thus, every space we enter becomes a silent reminder of the origin. Those who listen to the word to its very last echo recognize that the destination of the journey was always already hidden in its first sound.
