# Soil care and regeneration

Here are the most important findings and methods, divided into the care of the home garden and the large-scale greening of desert landscapes.

## Part 1: Maintaining and improving topsoil in the garden
Topsoil is the most valuable layer in the garden. The following measures are crucial to keeping it healthy and fertile:

* **Covering the soil (mulching):** Bare soil dries out and loses nutrients. A layer of grass clippings, leaves, or straw protects the soil and provides new nutrients as it decomposes.
* **Add organic material:** Regular applications of mature compost or organic fertilizers (such as horn shavings) build up the vital humus.
* **Avoid digging (no-dig):** Deep digging destroys the natural soil layers and microorganisms. It is better to only loosen the soil gently (e.g., with a garden fork) and leave the heavy work to the earthworms.
* **Utilize green manure:** Empty beds should be sown with plants such as clover or lupins. They loosen the soil deeply, shade it, and enrich it with nitrogen (especially in the case of clover).
* **Prevent compaction:** To keep the soil porous and oxygen-rich, beds should not be walked on and heavy machinery should be avoided.
* **Diversity instead of monoculture:** Crop rotation and mixed cropping prevent the soil from being depleted in one direction, which also helps prevent soil-borne diseases.

## Part 2: Reversing desertification (Regenerative agriculture)
Turning dead desert soil back into fertile land requires building a completely new ecosystem to break the cycle of desiccation and erosion:

* **Retaining water in the soil (water harvesting):** The most important step. Through earthworks such as swales along contour lines or small planting holes, rare rainwater is captured so that it can seep into the ground instead of running off.
* **Protection from wind and sun:** Robust pioneer plants (e.g., acacias) are planted as windbreaks. They also provide shade, which drastically reduces soil temperature and evaporation.
* **Building up biomass and nutrients:** Nitrogen-fixing plants are cultivated and regularly cut back. The clippings are left on the ground ("chop and drop") to form mulch and later humus. Biochar also helps to retain water and nutrients in the sand.
* **Holistic Management:** Large herds of animals are kept close together on open land for very short periods. They break up compacted soil, trample seeds, and fertilize the earth. Afterwards, the land has months to recover and grow.
* **Establishing an agroforestry ecosystem:** The goal is a self-sustaining system of trees, shrubs, and ground cover. A closed canopy protects the soil, while deep roots pump water and nutrients upwards for shallower plants.
