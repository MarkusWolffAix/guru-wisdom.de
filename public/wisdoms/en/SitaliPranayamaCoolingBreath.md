# The Cooling Breath - Sitali Pranayama
## For Refreshment and Mental Clarity

When the summer sun is high in the sky and the world is bathed in a shimmering sea of heat, we feel the fire not only on our skin. Our minds, too, can easily overheat on such days. We become restless, perhaps a little more irritable, and our thoughts feel heavy and sluggish. It is the time when we long for shade and cool water in the outside world—and when we are called to create an oasis of freshness within ourselves.

From the ancient traditions of yoga, a wonderful technique reaches out to us for this: **Sitali Pranayama**, the cooling breath. This gentle practice is like an inner waterfall that not only cools us physically but also soothes emotional heat.

### The Secret of Sitali

The word "Sitali" comes from Sanskrit and literally means "the cooling" or "the calming." Unlike most other pranayama exercises, where we breathe exclusively through the nose, in Sitali we use the mouth for inhalation.

By rolling the tongue into a moist tube, we guide the incoming air over this cool surface. The air is moistened and its temperature lowered before it enters our body. It's as if we are drinking pure, liquid moonlight energy.

### A Loving Guide for Your Practice

Take a few minutes on a hot afternoon. Find a shady spot, perhaps under an old tree or by an open window where a gentle breeze is blowing.

**1. Arriving**
Sit comfortably and upright. Let your shoulders gently drop, close your eyes, and feel the connection to the earth beneath you. Breathe in and out naturally through your nose a few times. Lovingly accept the warmth of your body without trying to change it.

**2. The Shape**
Now stick your tongue out a little and roll the sides upwards so that it looks like a small tube or a delicate, folded leaf.

*(A little tip: About a third of all people are genetically unable to roll their tongue. That's perfectly fine! In this case, use "Sitkari": Gently place the tip of your tongue against the back of your upper front teeth and open your lips in a slight smile. The cooling effect is the same.)*

**3. The Cooling (The Inhalation)**
Now breathe in slowly, deeply, and audibly through the tongue tube (or through your slightly parted teeth). Consciously feel the air glide over your tongue. Notice the wonderful coolness that spreads through your mouth, your throat, and finally your chest.

**4. The Release (The Exhalation)**
Close your lips gently and exhale slowly and silently through your nose. Imagine that with this exhale, you are simply releasing all excess heat, tension, and fiery thoughts into the surrounding environment.

**5. The Rhythm of Silence**
Repeat this gentle cycle for a few minutes—about 9 to 12 breaths, or simply for as long as it feels refreshing. Let your breath flow like gentle, cool waves on a tranquil lake. Nothing needs to be forced.

### The Afterglow Phase

When you are ready to end the exercise, let your breath flow naturally in and out through your nose again. Keep your eyes closed for a moment.

Do you feel the difference? Sitali often leaves you with a feeling of deep contentment and a remarkably clear, still mind. It reminds us that we are not helplessly at the mercy of external circumstances. We literally carry the tool to create peace and coolness within us on our tongues.

Take this inner freshness with you for the rest of your day. Namaste.
