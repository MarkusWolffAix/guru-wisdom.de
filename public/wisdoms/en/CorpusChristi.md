# The Mystery of Corpus Christi
## In the Footsteps of Juliana of Liège

Corpus Christi is a feast that, at first glance, often appears to be a purely traditional, strictly Catholic custom. But those who look more closely discover at its core a deep, fascinating mysticism that goes far beyond dogma.

The term itself comes from Middle High German. *“Vrône”* means “that which concerns the Lord” or simply “Lord,” and *“lîcham”* stands for the living body. It is thus literally the feast of the “Body of the Lord.” In tradition, this day celebrates the abiding presence of the divine in the physical—specifically in the sacrament of the Eucharist, the consecrated bread.

### The Vision of Juliana of Liège

The origin of this feast has a strong, tangible connection to our immediate home region. The origin of Corpus Christi can be traced back to Juliana of Liège, an Augustinian nun who lived in the 13th century. Beginning in 1209, she repeatedly experienced a remarkable vision: she saw the radiant full moon, but with a dark spot in one place.

In her mystical vision, it was revealed to her that this moon symbolized the Church as a whole, and the dark spot represented the absence of a very specific feast—a feast dedicated purely to the veneration of the physical presence of the Divine in the Blessed Sacrament. Through her persistent advocacy and tireless devotion, Pope Urban IV finally established Corpus Christi as a universal feast of the Church in 1264.

### Places of Power and Pilgrimage

There is something profoundly powerful about not only studying historical and spiritual roots in books, but also walking them in their physical presence. Liège and its surrounding area offer three key locations that trace the life of Juliana and her legacy:

1. **The Sanctuary on Mont Cornillon (Sanctuaire de Sainte-Julienne de Cornillon)**

This is the true origin. Here, Juliana grew up in the Augustinian convent and, at the age of 16, had her first defining lunar vision. The place still exudes a monastic tranquility and offers an ideal space for meditative connection with one's own inner peace.

2. **The Basilica of Saint Martin in Liège**

This imposing basilica stands in the heart of Liège. Its historical significance stems from the fact that the very first Feast of Corpus Christi in world history was celebrated here in 1246. It is the place where Juliana's inner vision was first visibly expressed to the outside world of a community.


2. **The Basilica of Saint Martin in Liège**

This imposing basilica stands in the very center of Liège. 3. **The Ruins of Villers-la-Ville**

Juliana's path was not without its obstacles. She was forced to flee during her life and eventually found refuge with the Cistercians. After her death in 1258, she was buried in Villers-la-Ville at her own request. The imposing, atmospheric ruins of this abbey still convey the ephemeral nature of physical architecture and the enduring spirit that such individuals bring into the world.

### The Universal Essence of the Procession

The most visible aspect of Corpus Christi today is the procession. Normally, the Blessed Sacrament—the consecrated host—rests protected within the church, in the tabernacle. On this special feast day, however, the Blessed Sacrament is taken from its protected space and carried through the streets, villages, and across the vast fields.

The most visible aspect of Corpus Christi today is the procession. Normally, the Blessed Sacrament—the consecrated host—rests safely within the church, in the tabernacle. On this particular feast day, however, the Blessed Sacrament is taken from its protected space and carried through the streets, villages, and across the open fields.


### The Universal Essence of the Procession Within it lies a universal, spiritual symbolism that can be seamlessly applied to our own lives: The divine, the sacred, and inner wisdom don't exist only behind thick walls or in the absolute silence of meditation. The true test of our practice is carrying this accumulated peace, our own inner "holy of holies," into the often loud, restless, and demanding world of our daily lives, and thereby penetrating the seemingly ordinary.
