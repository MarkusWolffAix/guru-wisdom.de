# The Mystery of Corpus Christi
## Following in the footsteps of Juliana of Liège

Corpus Christi is a feast that at first glance often appears to be a purely traditional, strictly Catholic custom. But those who look more closely discover at its core a deep, fascinating mysticism that goes far beyond dogma. 

The term itself originates from Middle High German. *"Vrône"* means "that which concerns the Lord" or simply "Lord," and *"lîcham"* stands for the living body. It is therefore literally the feast of the "Body of the Lord." Traditionally, this day celebrates the abiding presence of the divine in the physical realm—specifically in the sacrament of the Eucharist, the consecrated bread.

### The vision of Juliana of Liège

The origin of this festival has a strong, tangible connection to our immediate home region. Its origins trace back to Juliana of Liège, an Augustinian nun who lived in the 13th century. From the year 1209 onwards, she repeatedly experienced a remarkable vision: she saw the radiant full moon, which, however, had a dark spot in one place. 

In her mystical vision, it was revealed to her that this moon symbolized the Church as a whole, and the dark spot represented the absence of a very specific feast—a feast dedicated purely to the veneration of the physical presence of the Divine in the Holy Eucharist. Through her persistent advocacy and tireless devotion, Pope Urban IV finally elevated Corpus Christi to a universal feast day of the Church in 1264.

### Places of power and pilgrimage

There is something profoundly powerful about not just studying historical and spiritual roots in books, but physically walking through them. Liège and its surrounding area offer three key locations that span the life story of Juliana and her legacy:

1. **The Sanctuary on Mont Cornillon (Sanctuaire de Sainte-Julienne de Cornillon)**
   This is the absolute origin. Here, Juliana grew up in the Augustinian convent and, at the age of 16, had her first defining lunar vision. To this day, the place exudes a monastic tranquility and offers an ideal space to connect meditatively with one's own inner stillness.

2. **The Basilica of Saint Martin in Liège**
   This imposing basilica stands right in the heart of Liège. Its historical significance lies in the fact that the very first Corpus Christi procession in world history was celebrated here in 1246. It is the place where Juliana's inner vision was first visibly conveyed to the outside world of a community.

3. **The ruins of Villers-la-Ville**
   Juliana's path was not without its obstacles. She was forced to flee during her life and eventually found refuge with the Cistercians. After her death in 1258, she was buried in Villers-la-Ville, according to her own wishes. The vast, atmospheric ruins of this abbey today convey how transient material architecture is, yet how eternal the spirit that such people bring into the world remains.

### The universal essence of the procession

The most visible aspect of Corpus Christi today is the procession. Normally, the Blessed Sacrament – the consecrated host – rests protected inside the church, in the tabernacle. On this one feast day, however, the Blessed Sacrament is taken from its protected space and carried through the streets, through the villages, and across the wide fields.

Concealed within it is a universal, spiritual symbolism that can be seamlessly applied to our own lives: The divine, the sacred, and inner wisdom do not exist only behind thick walls or in the absolute silence of meditation. The true test of our practice is to carry this accumulated peace, our own inner "holy of holies," into the often noisy, restless, and demanding world of our daily lives and thereby permeate the seemingly ordinary.
