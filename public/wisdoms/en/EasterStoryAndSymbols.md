# Easter

## History, origin and symbols

### 1. The biblical Easter story
Easter forms the foundation of the Christian faith and celebrates the victory of life over death. The story encompasses the last days of Jesus:
* **Palm Sunday:** Jesus enters Jerusalem on a donkey like a king and is cheered by the people with palm branches.
* **Maundy Thursday:** At the Last Supper, Jesus shares bread and wine as symbols of his body and blood. That same night, he is betrayed and arrested by Judas Iscariot in the Garden of Gethsemane.
* **Good Friday:** Jesus is condemned to death by the Roman governor Pontius Pilate and crucified on the hill of Golgotha. 
* **Holy Saturday:** The day of rest in the grave, of mourning and silence.
* **Easter Sunday:** Women discover the empty tomb. An angel announces the resurrection of Jesus, who then appears to his disciples.

### 2. The gravestone and its meaning
A central element of the Easter story is the heavy stone that was rolled in front of the rock tomb. It carries two levels of meaning:
* **Historically and practically:** In ancient Judea, such a boulder protected the tomb from wild animals and grave robbers. In Jesus' case, it was also sealed with a Roman seal and guarded, for fear that the disciples might steal the body.
* **Theologically and symbolically:** On Good Friday, the stone represents the irrevocable end, the coldness of death, and the separation from the living. Its removal on Easter morning reverses the narrative: It did not happen so that Jesus could emerge, but so that people could see into the empty tomb. The stone thus becomes the ultimate proof of the resurrection and symbolizes that divine power overcomes even the most formidable earthly obstacles.

### 3. The origin of the word "Easter" and the meaning of the "Albe"
The exact origin of the word is not entirely clear, but there are three main theories, the last of which is closely linked to an old clothing tradition:
* **Dawn/East (most likely theory):** Derived from the Indo-European root **aus-* (to shine). It refers to the light of dawn, at which the empty tomb was discovered, and the direction east, towards which early Christians faced when praying.
* **Spring goddess “Ostara” (myth):** A widespread but historically unsubstantiated theory states that the festival was named after a Germanic spring goddess.
* **The translation theory surrounding the Feast of Baptism:** In the early Church, many people were baptized at Easter. These newly baptized individuals wore an **alb** as a symbol of purity, innocence, and new life. The term derives from the Latin word *albus* (white) or *tunica alba* (white undergarment). This white garment was worn throughout Easter week. Researchers suspect that the Latin word *alba* (white) was mistakenly confused over time with the term for "dawn," from which the name of the feast may have been derived.

### 4. Good Friday and Holy Saturday
The prefix **"Kar-"** comes from the Old High German word *kara* , which means "lament", "sorrow" or "mourning".
* **Good Friday:** Literally the "Friday of Mourning". It commemorates the crucifixion and is a legally protected "silent holiday" in Germany (e.g., with a ban on dancing).
* **Holy Saturday:** The "Saturday of Mourning". It is the day of rest in the grave without festive church services or altar decorations. 
* *(Note: The term "Easter Saturday," often used colloquially, actually refers to the Saturday after Easter in the church calendar.)*

### 5. The Easter Egg
The egg as an Easter symbol combines practical, religious, and pagan roots:
* **The practical reason was** that eggs were forbidden during the 40-day Lenten fast. To preserve the eggs still laid by the hens, they were hard-boiled. To distinguish them from raw eggs, they were dyed with plant juices.
* **Christian symbolism:** The egg represents the tomb of Jesus – rigid and lifeless on the outside, but new life bursts forth from within.
* **Universal symbol:** Thousands of years before Christianity, the egg represented spring, fertility and new beginnings in many cultures.

### 6. The Easter Bunny
The idea that a rabbit brings the Easter eggs is a mixture of natural observations and Protestant tradition:
* **Fertility symbol:** Rabbits are the first animals to have abundant offspring in spring and were often seen close to people in the fields during this time of year.
* **Protestant invention:** Since Protestants rejected the Catholic blessing of eggs in church, they invented the child-friendly story of the Easter Bunny in the 17th century to explain the sudden appearance of the eggs.
* **Commercial breakthrough:** Originally, depending on the region, other animals were believed to bring eggs (such as the stork or fox). The rabbit finally became established worldwide in the 19th century due to the success of illustrated children's books and the emerging production of chocolate bunnies.
