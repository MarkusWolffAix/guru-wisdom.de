# Creation myths of the world
## The universal parallels of the origin of the world

It is a fascinating phenomenon of comparative mythology that geographically and temporally completely isolated cultures – from the Polynesians to the ancient Greeks, and on to the Egyptians and Sumerians – developed almost identical narratives to explain the origin of the cosmos. 

At the heart of all these myths lies the archetype of the **"World Parents"** . The core motifs can be divided into five universal phases:

### 1. The original embrace (The original state)
In almost all of these traditions, the world does not initially exist as empty space, but as a massive, inseparable unity of heaven and earth, personified as loving or procreative deities.
* **Māori:** Papatūānuku (Earth Mother) and Ranginui (Sky Father) lie in an eternal, close embrace.
* **Greek:** Gaia (Earth Mother) and Uranus (Heaven Father) form the same oppressive, permanent union.
* **Egyptian:** Geb (earth god) and Nut (sky goddess) are also inextricably linked (here only with reversed gender roles).
* **Sumerian:** Ki (Earth) and An (Heaven) initially exist together as an undivided, gigantic world mountain.

### 2. Imprisonment in the Dark
The direct result of this tight embrace, according to the myths, is the absolute lack of space and light for life.
* **Māori:** The god children vegetate in complete darkness and confinement between the bodies of their parents.
* **Greek:** Uranus forces their children (the Titans) deep into the dark belly of the earth (Tartarus), where they are imprisoned and cannot emerge into the light.

### 3. The violent separation
To create space for evolution, light, and life, the unity of the world parents must be torn apart by a radical, often violent act of one of their sons. The supporting element (forest or air) separates above and below.
* **Māori:** Tāne Mahuta (God of the Forest) uses the strength of his legs and arms to lift the sky and separate the parents.
* **Greek:** The Titan Cronus brutally separates heaven and earth by emasculating his father Uranus with a sickle.
* **Egyptian:** Shu (god of the air) pushes himself between his parents and lifts the sky (Nut) from the earth (Geb) with his hands.
* **Sumerian:** Enlil (god of the wind) splits the world mountain into heaven and earth with elemental force.

### 4. The Civil War and the Division of the World
After space was created, the myths describe a period of familial chaos. The children of the gods (the forces of nature) fight for supremacy and divide the world empires among themselves. This explains the often hostile nature of the elements.
* **Māori:** The weather god Tāwhirimātea is angry about the separation and sends storms to punish his brothers (sea, forest, harvest). An eternal cycle of erosion, storms, and deforestation begins.
* **Greek:** The Olympian gods overthrow the Titans, whereupon Zeus, Poseidon, and Hades divide the domains of heaven, sea, and underworld among themselves. Here, too, conflicts among the gods shape the extreme weather and natural events.

### 5. Man as material of the earth
In these myths, the creation of mankind is not described as a magical spell cast from nothing, but as a manual act in which man is formed directly from the physical substance of the earth.
* **Māori:** The forest god Tāne forms the very first woman (Hine-ahu-one) from the red clay of Papatūānuku and breathes life into her.
* **Greek:** The Titan Prometheus kneads the first humans from the clay and water of the earth (Gaia).

---
**The anthropological conclusion:**
This comprehensive examination of commonalities makes it clear that myths are not random fantasies. They are the result of universal human observations: the sky "fertilizes" the earth with rain, whereupon the earth gives birth to life. Plants (the children) need light and space between earth and sky to grow. The parallels between Māori, Greeks, Egyptians, and Sumerians show that humanity, regardless of continent or era, has used the same logical and poetic language to explain the architecture of nature.
