# The mnemonic device
## From a detour to self-discovery

We often use the word "mnemonic device" casually in everyday life to refer to small learning aids or mnemonic devices. However, its true origin contains a profound metaphor about learning, respecting our own limitations, and the ways in which we overcome them.

To understand this, we must return to a time when animals were our everyday companions. Donkeys have a reputation for being stubborn. But this supposed "stubbornness" is, in reality, a deeply ingrained, very clever survival instinct. A donkey will strictly refuse to wade through a small body of water or a stream, even if the water is only ankle-deep. The reason for this lies in the nature of the water: because of the reflection on the surface, the animal cannot see how deep the bottom is. Where the donkey cannot see for sure what it is stepping on, it stops. It does not trust the unknown. 

To avoid forcing the animal – which was hardly possible anyway – people built small, very simple bridges over these waterways: the so-called "donkey bridge". It was a small detour, an artificial aid that allowed the animal to cross the obstacle with dry feet and, above all, without fear. 

That's exactly how our minds work. Sometimes we stand before a piece of information, a formula, or an abstract concept like a donkey before a stream. Our minds refuse to take the direct route; they can't find a foothold, and the memory remains hazy. The mnemonic device in our thinking—be it a rhyme, an absurd image, or a short story—is this artificial detour. It relieves our minds of the weight of pure facts and builds a safe, easily navigable path to true understanding. The wisdom of the mnemonic device, therefore, lies not in the donkey's stupidity, but in the bridge-builder's wise care. It's perfectly fine if the direct route is sometimes too difficult; we are allowed to create detours to reach our destination.

But this already beautiful story reveals yet another, far deeper dimension when we look more closely at the cause of the fear: the reflection in the water. 

What if the donkey not only lacked knowledge about water, but the reflection itself carried the real message? If you mirror the word "donkey," the word "read" appears. 

This wonderful observation is a masterful play on words that reveals a philosophical truth: The donkey, gazing into the mirror of his own fear, finds there the call to knowledge. *Read.* The water, which, through its reflection, obscures his view of the bottom and paralyzes him, conceals no danger at all, but rather an invitation. When he pauses, looks into it, and recognizes himself there, ignorance transforms into understanding. "Reading"—whether in books, in the patterns of our own reactions, or in the realities of nature—is the key to dissolving the obscurity. 

Those who learn to read and understand the unknown soon no longer need artificial bridges, because they realize that the waters of life are not as deep and threatening as they first appear. External aids thus become an inner transformation through knowledge. Looking into the murky water becomes reading one's own soul.

### The poetry of the Bridge

**[Verse 1]**
One of them stops at the edge of the stream,
The water doesn't paint the bottom.
Not a step if eyes cannot understand,
No coercion, no mockery, no noisy house.

**[Pre-Chorus]**
Then you place boards over questions,
a path of wood and confidence.
You don't have to risk every abyss,
when a detour is safer.

**[Chorus]**
Build yourself a bridge when the path becomes blurred,
a short sentence that will take you across.
What at first appears to be a weakness will carry you through time:
A detour can be the beginning of your clarity.

**[Verse 2]**
A rhyme preserves what numbers cannot.
A picture makes unfamiliar things seem closer.
Stories can open doors,
where previously there was only a question mark.

**[Pre-Chorus]**
And if there are pictures in the water,
to make fear greater than the truth,
Then learn to read the surface,
and listen to what your doubts say.

**[Chorus]**
Build yourself a bridge when the path becomes blurred,
a short sentence that will take you across.
What at first appears to be a weakness will carry you through time:
A detour can be the beginning of your clarity.

**[Bridge]**
Donkey in the mirror, reading in the light,
The foreign may not be hidden.
What's holding you back is calling out very softly:
Understand the path, and it will become vast.

**[Final Chorus]**
Build yourself a bridge until your view expands,
until you walk fearlessly through deeper water.
Every boundary you respect becomes an opportunity:
The detour leads inwards, and time leads inwards.

**[Outro]**
It becomes quiet at the edge of the stream.
You read what the water wants to say.


### English Translation: The Lyric of the Bridge

**[Verse 1]**
At the edge of the stream, someone stands still,
the water does not paint the bottom.
No step, when eyes understand nothing,
no force, no mockery, no loud house.

**[Pre-Chorus]**
Then one lays boards over questions,
a path of wood and confidence.
One doesn't have to brave every abyss,
when a detour speaks more safely.

**[Chorus]**
Build yourself a bridge when the path blurs,
a small sentence that takes you across.
What first looks like weakness carries you through time:
A detour can be the beginning of your clarity.

**[Verse 2]**
A rhyme preserves what numbers keep silent,
an image makes strange things close.
Stories can reveal doors,
where before there was only a question mark.

**[Pre-Chorus]**
And when pictures lie in the water,
making the fear greater than the truth,
then learn to read the surface,
and hear what your doubts say.

**[Chorus]**
Build yourself a bridge when the path blurs,
a small sentence that takes you across.
What first looks like weakness carries you through time:
A detour can be the beginning of your clarity.

**[Bridge]**
DONKEY in the mirror, READ in the light,
the unfamiliar perhaps does not hide.
What keeps you back calls very softly:
Understand the way, then it will become wider.

**[Final Chorus]**
Build yourself a bridge until your gaze widens,
until you stride through deeper water without fear.
Every boundary you respect becomes a possibility:
The detour leads inwards, and inwards leads time.

**[Outro]**
At the edge of the stream, it grows quiet.
You read what the water wants to say.x
