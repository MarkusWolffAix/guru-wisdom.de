# The Discovery of Zero
## The Origin of Nothingness

The invention or "discovery" of zero in India was not the work of a single person on a specific day, but rather a gradual historical development. However, there is one central figure to whom the mathematical definition of zero is attributed: **Brahmagupta** .

## Important milestones and scholars
Here are the most important scholars and institutions involved in this revolutionary development:

* **The Bakhshali Manuscript:** This ancient document contains the oldest known physical evidence of the use of an explicit zero symbol in India. Researchers have dated parts of the text to the 3rd or 4th century. It used a small dot ( *bindu* ), from which the ring-shaped symbol we know today later developed.
* **Aryabhata (c. 500 AD):** This important astronomer and mathematician did not yet use an explicit symbol for zero, but he worked with a decimal place-value system. This system was conceptually based on the idea of a "void" and paved the way for Brahmagupta.
* **Brahmagupta (c. 628 CE):** He is considered the mathematician who first defined zero as an independent, fully-fledged number, and not merely as a placeholder. In his astronomical and mathematical work *Brahmasphutasiddhanta* , he was the first to formulate the fundamental rules of calculation for zero (e.g., 1 - 1 = 0, 1 + 0 = 1, 1 x 0 = 0). He called the concept **Shunya** (Sanskrit for "emptiness").

## The visual and symbolic representation of zero

To illustrate the profound significance of zero in India, the following elements can be visualized:

* **The Indian scholar:** He represents the great mathematicians like Brahmagupta or Aryabhata, who formulated and made the concept of zero calculable. The mathematical formulas in the air (e.g., 1 - 1 = 0) stand for this achievement.
* **The Eye of Knowledge:** At its center, zero manifests as a luminous eye. It symbolizes knowledge, cosmic consciousness, and the ability to penetrate the essence of emptiness.
* **The Golden Egg (Hiranyagarbha):** The zero encloses a radiant golden egg. In Indian philosophy, this is the "cosmic egg," the origin of all being and the potential for creation that arises from emptiness.
* **Love and Emptiness:** The zero is surrounded by hearts and gentle light. The Sanskrit inscription on the left reads **"Shunya"** (शून्य – emptiness/zero), while the inscription on the right means **"Prema"** (प्रेमा – love). This illustrates the idea that zero is not just a number, but a state of wholeness and universal connection.

---

## Conclusion
While earlier, sometimes unknown scholars (such as the authors of the Bakhshali manuscript) provided the concept of the void with a symbol, it was **Brahmagupta** who gave zero its mathematical rules and thus made it a real number.
