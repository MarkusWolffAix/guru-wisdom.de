# The Little Sun and Her Big Celestial Clock
## How Tilted Uranus Became the Clock Face of the Cosmos

On a beautiful cosmic morning, the sun blinked fully awake for the first time into the darkness. She felt her own warmth, beamed happily, and looked around curiously. There she saw all the planets, orbiting around her like colorful marbles.

"Ah, how lovely," thought the sun, smiling proudly. "Everything is dancing in a circle around me!"

For quite a while, she simply watched the joyful dance. But eventually, her curiosity grew even more intense. She wanted to know how long each dance lasted, when the next one began, and exactly how the vast space around her worked.

"Hmm," murmured the sun, frowning her luminous brow. "To observe all of this properly, I would need to be able to measure time. But... oh dear! I don't even have a clock!"


Searching, she let her warm gaze wander across the cosmos. Very close by, the enormous Jupiter orbited. "He would certainly be easy to see," the Sun mused. But because she herself was still a rather young, playful star, and the mighty Jupiter seemed far too heavy and imposing, she preferred to look a little further out into the blue chill.

Out there, in seventh place in the great planetary line, floated icy Uranus. He had a very special, pitch-black ring around his belly – the epsilon ring. But unfortunately, Uranus, like all the other planets, kept his ring completely flat, so that from the side, the Sun could only see a tiny, narrow line.

"I can't read it like that!" cried the Sun. Without further ado, she extended a long, golden beam of light, gently nudged Uranus on the side, and simply tipped him over!

Uranus tumbled a bit until he lay comfortably on his side. And lo and behold: Suddenly, its large black ring pointed directly at the sun, like a giant, round clock face!

"Oh, how magnificent!" exclaimed the sun. And as she looked even closer, she discovered a true marvel: The ring wasn't rigid at all, but breathed in time with the clock. Sometimes it contracted tightly, sometimes it expanded wide, just like the beating heart of a fine pocket watch. And to ensure that not a single speck of ice or stone got out of place, two tiny moons—the diligent German Shepherds Cordelia and Ophelia—whizzed along the inside and outside of the ring. If a stone nudged too far inward, Cordelia gently pushed it back. If one tried to escape outward, Ophelia skillfully stopped it.

*Tick-tock, tick-tock*, went the giant clockwork mechanism out there in the empty space.

The sun beamed from ear to ear. Now she not only had the most beautiful and largest station clock in the entire universe, but with every single tick she understood a little bit better how mysterious and wonderful the sky around her was.
