# Greek mythology

## The Saga of Uranus, Gaia and Cronus

The story of the Greek gods begins with a dramatic tale of creation, oppression, and bloody generational conflicts. Here is the unabridged summary of events from the origins of the cosmos to the rise of the Olympian gods.

### 1. The Beginning: Out of Chaos
In the beginning, there was only **chaos** , a yawning abyss. From this arose **Gaia** , the personified Earth and primordial mother. She brought forth from within herself **Uranus** , the star-strewn sky, so that it might protectively embrace her and serve as the eternal seat of the gods.

### 2. A problematic association
Powerful beings emerged from the constant union of Uranus and Gaia, but the relationship between father and children was poisoned from the beginning:

* **The 12 Titans:** Enormous, humanoid figures (including Kronos, Rhea and Okeanos).
* **The Cyclopes:** Three one-eyed giants and gifted smiths.
* **The Hecatoncheires:** Three monsters, each with 100 arms and 50 heads.

Uranus particularly detested the Cyclopes and Hecatoncheires because of their appearance and strength. He cast them back into **Tartarus** (the deepest underworld within the body of Gaia) and forbade them ever to see the light of day.

### 3. The revenge of Mother Earth and the fall of the sky
Gaia suffered excruciating pain from the children trapped within her. In her anger, she forged a colossal sickle from adamas (grey steel) and incited her children to revolt. Only the youngest and most cunning Titan, **Kronos** , possessed the courage to act.

When Uranus lay upon Gaia again that night, Cronus attacked from ambush and **castrated his father** with a sickle. He threw the severed body parts into the sea. From this violent act, other beings were born:
* The blood that dripped onto the earth gave birth to the **Giants** and **Erinyes** (Avenging Goddesses).
* From the foam that formed in the sea around the genitals, **Aphrodite** , the goddess of love, was born.

Heaven was now separated from Earth. But the deposed Uranus, dying (or retreating), uttered a curse: Cronus would one day suffer the same fate and be overthrown by his own son.

### 4. The Reign of the Titans and the Dark Shadow
Kronos rose to become the undisputed ruler of the world and took his Titan sister **Rhea** as his wife. However, he broke his promise to Gaia: he did not free the ugly Cyclopes and Hecatoncheires, but kept them imprisoned in Tartarus.

The constant, dark shadow of his father's prophecy hung over Kronos. Consumed by paranoia and a lust for power, he chose a drastic method: every time Rhea gave birth to a child, he snatched it from her arms and **swallowed it whole** . Since the children were immortal gods, they did not die but continued to live on in their father's dark stomach. This fate befell **Hestia, Demeter, Hera, Hades, and Poseidon** .

### 5. Rheia's List and the Stone
Rhea was desperate. When she became pregnant with her sixth child, she turned to her parents, Gaia and Uranus, for help. On their advice, she fled to the island **of Crete** , where she secretly gave birth to her son **Zeus** in a dark cave.

When Kronos demanded another newborn a short time later, Rhea wrapped a **large stone** in baby diapers and presented it to her husband. Blinded by fear and greed, Kronos swallowed the stone whole, firmly believing he had averted the danger.

Zeus grew up hidden away on Crete. He was nourished by the milk of the mythical goat Amalthea, while nature spirits (the Curetes) drowned out his baby cries with loud clashing of weapons and dances.

### 6. The Return of Zeus and the Titanomachy
When Zeus had grown into a powerful god, he returned. With the help of the clever Titaness Metis, he secretly mixed a **vomit** into Cronus's nectar. Cronus, in agony, first vomited up the stone and then all his swallowed siblings – unharmed and now fully grown.

The freed siblings joined Zeus to take revenge. A ten-year, world-shattering war ensued: the **Titanomachy** .
* Kronos and the ancient Titans fought from Mount Othrys.
* Zeus, his siblings, and new allies fought from Mount Olympus.

Zeus was clever enough to free the Cyclopes and Hecatoncheires, imprisoned by Cronus, from Tartarus. In gratitude, the Cyclopes forged Zeus's most powerful weapon: **lightning and thunder** .

In the end, Zeus triumphed with the immense power of his allies. He cast Cronus and the Titans loyal to him deep into **Tartarus** , where they were now imprisoned forever – guarded by the hundred-armed Hecatoncheires. With this victory, the age of the Titans ended and the reign of Zeus and the Olympian gods began.
