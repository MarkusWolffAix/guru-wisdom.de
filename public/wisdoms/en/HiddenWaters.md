# The Hidden Current
## From the Unity of the Forest to the Depths of the Wurm

There are forces that work in secret, and only through their convergence do they create a foundation that supports the visible world. Deep beneath the cobblestone streets of Aachen's Frankenberger Quarter, such a union takes place, one that is far more than a geological phenomenon. It is a reflection of the inner duality of spirit and matter.

Two streams flow toward each other here. The Beverbach, whose name derives from the beaver, represents the archaic builder. It stands for the worldly, the tangible, the creation of structures, and the laying of solid foundations in the physical world. Flowing against it is the Gillesbach, named after Saint Giles. It carries the spiritual dimension, evoking contemplation, inner guidance, and quiet trust in a higher order.

The fascinating aspect of this meeting is the realization of their true origin. The Beverbach and the Gillesbach both originate from the same living, breathing nature: the Aachen Forest. They are born of the same earth and fed by the same rain. For a time, they take different paths through the landscape, seemingly fulfilling separate tasks. But in truth, they were never separate.

Deep beneath the earth, they finally unite to form the Wurm. In this primeval name, the deep "Ur" (primordial) rests, enclosed by the initials W.M., like a protected core. This reunification of form and spirit does not occur with a loud roar on the surface, but in the silent depths. It serves as a reminder that earthly creation and spiritual orientation are not opposites, but two streams from the same source that inevitably find each other again.
