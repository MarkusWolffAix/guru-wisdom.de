# The numinous
## Rudolf Otto's Path to the Completely Different

There are moments in human experience that defy all rational understanding. When we stand beneath the infinity of the starry sky, feel the oppressive silence of a centuries-old cathedral, or gaze into the raw power of nature, we cross the threshold of the ineffable. The German theologian and philosopher of religion Rudolf Otto coined the term " **numinous"** (derived from the Latin * *numen** , meaning "divine power" or "hint") to describe this specific, profound feeling. It is the experience of the absolutely sacred, the "wholly other."

### The Cartographer of the Saint

Rudolf Otto (1869–1937), who spent many formative years as a scholar in Marburg, was not merely an armchair thinker. Extensive travels took him between 1911 and 1912 to India, China, Japan, and the Middle East, among other places. He encountered a wide variety of religious traditions—Hinduism, Buddhism, and Islam—with deep respect and keen observation. His search was not for the confirmation of rigid dogmas, but rather for the universally unifying element in the history of human thought.

This quest culminated in 1917 with the publication of his seminal work *, "The Holy."* In a time marked by the upheavals of the First World War, and when pure Enlightenment reason was reaching its limits, Otto revolutionized the understanding of spirituality. Before his work, Western philosophy and theology often reduced the divine to the epitome of the absolutely good and rational. Otto vehemently disagreed: he recognized that the sacred possesses an irrational core that extends far beyond human moral categories.

### The anatomy of numinous experience

The numinous defies logic, but can be phenomenologically described as a paradoxical experience that literally tears the soul apart. Otto identified two opposing yet inextricably linked poles of this encounter:

* **Mysterium Tremendum:** This is the earth-shattering awe before the overwhelming power ( *Majestas* ) and the burning, intoxicating vitality ( *Energia* ) of the divine. It is an infinite, almost menacing presence that makes one feel diminished.
* **The Mystery of Fascination:** At the same time, this "wholly Other" exerts an irresistible attraction, grace, and profound delight. It awakens an indelible longing within us.

When a person enters the realm of the numinous, what Otto calls the **creaturely feeling** inevitably arises. It is the pure, immediate awareness of one's own limitations—the feeling of being "dust and ashes." Paradoxically, however, this is not a degrading humiliation, but a profoundly liberating self-awareness in the face of absolute majesty.

### The universal premonition

But how do we recognize the sacred when it is so incomprehensible and, as the "wholly Other" ( *Mirum* ), lies entirely outside our everyday experience of being? Otto attributed to humankind a special, universal capacity: the *sensus numinis* . Through the principle of divination—intuitive sensing or intuition—we are able to perceive the sacred when it breaks through into our reality. Whether a Zen monk in meditation, a Christian mystic in contemplation, or a person standing awestruck before a majestic mountain range—the fundamental feeling of being touched by something greater remains the same.

### Ways to delve deeper: Literature

Those who wish to explore the architecture of the saint themselves and understand the nuances of this philosophy will find valuable guidance in the following works:

**Primary works by Rudolf Otto:**
* **“The Sacred. On the Irrational in the Idea of the Divine and its Relation to the Rational” (1917):** His undisputed masterpiece, in which he unfolds the core concepts of the numinous and the *mysterium tremendum et fascinans* in a dense and interreligious manner.
* **"East-West Mysticism: Comparison and Differentiation for the Interpretation of Essence" (1926):** A testament to his cosmopolitan mind. Here, Otto compares the thoughts of the German mystic Meister Eckhart with those of the great Indian philosopher Shankara and demonstrates how the numinous transcends cultural boundaries.

**The heirs of his ideas:**
* **Mircea Eliade – “The Sacred and the Profane” (1957):** The historian of religion builds directly on Otto’s concept of the numinous and examines how people throughout history have structured space, time and nature to separate the sacred from the everyday.
* **Carl Gustav Jung – “Psychology and Religion” (1938):** The psychoanalyst adopted the concept of the numinous from Otto, but interpreted it primarily as the enormous, autonomous power of the unconscious and its archetypes, which can completely captivate people.

Rudolf Otto's philosophy and the literature he inspired serve as a lasting reminder that spirituality does not begin with sacred texts. It is rooted much deeper: in the archaic, breathtaking feeling of reality itself. It is the moment when the world takes on a shuddering, fascinating resonance.
