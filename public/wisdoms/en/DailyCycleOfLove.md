# The Breathing Day
## From Awakening to Beauty and Returning to Oneness

The daily rhythm of our lives can be seen as a profound, almost sacred cycle. Awakening in the morning is far more than simply opening our eyes; it is a conscious act of separation. We release ourselves from the deep, resting connection with the earth—that sheltered state of night—to enter the active vitality of the day.

The motivation for this daily awakening does not stem from mere fulfillment of duty, but from a devoted service to beauty. We rise to actively contribute to the growth of love in the world. The day becomes a canvas on which we co-create. This happens through the conscious experience and creation of "beautiful images." This can occur in the quiet contemplation of nature, in mindful interaction with our surroundings—like a measured, joyful "spring cleaning" that creates space for new beginnings—or in creative expression through art and music. We gather and create beauty to give it back to the world.

A central wisdom of this conscious daily routine lies in immediate inner hygiene. Everyday life inevitably brings unpleasant impressions, murky images, and negative thoughts. The art lies in not simply depositing these sensory impressions uncommented in the subconscious, but in addressing them *immediately* with love. It is a process of active transformation: We turn to the negative thoughts, envelop them with understanding, and thus transform them into love.

This continuous cleansing of consciousness is essential for our inner peace. For everything we don't resolve during the day casts a shadow over the evening. If we don't transform the unpleasant impressions immediately, we have to laboriously confront them before falling asleep in order to find peace again. However, if we deal with them directly, we pave the way for a completely unburdened return. In the evening, when the day's work is done, we sink back into tranquility. The circle closes and we become one with love and the earth again - supported and ready to draw new strength in our sleep.
