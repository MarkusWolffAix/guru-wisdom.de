# Rome in the mirror
## The inner return to love and peace

"All roads lead to Rome"—an old proverb deeply rooted in our cultural consciousness. It traditionally describes the external journey, the pursuit of a center of civilization, structure, and worldly power. Looking at the area within a radius of 888 kilometers around this ancient metropolis, we hear the city's name in a multitude of languages that characterize the Mediterranean and Europe: *Roma* in Italian and Spanish, *Rome* in French, *Rom* in German, *Rómi* in Greek, *Rim* in Croatian, *Romë* in Albanian, and *Erroma* in Basque.

But what happens when we walk this path not externally, but internally? What happens when we look at the names of this eternal realm as if in a mirror and simply reverse the words? A startling, almost mystical pattern is revealed – a spiritual path that transforms the harsh reality of an empire into the most delicate aspects of human consciousness.

**From world power to love: Roma and Cupid**
In its place of origin, in both Italian and Spanish, the city is called *Roma* . Reversing this word gives us *Amor* – all-encompassing love. The road to Rome is humanity's outward quest; the reversal of the word is the inner truth. Those who arrive at the center and turn their gaze inward will find no more stone monuments, but rather the essence of love.

**The Basque confirmation: Erroma and Amorre**
Even the ancient and isolated Basque language follows this pattern. *Erroma* spelled backwards becomes *Amorre* – a sound that inevitably evokes the Romance word for love. It shows that the essence of love itself resonates at the core of this center, transcending ancient linguistic boundaries.

**From external goals to inner peace: Rim and Mir**
In Croatian, the city's name is *Rim* . Reversing this sound yields *Mir* , the ancient Slavic word for peace. The realization is compelling: the true path doesn't end in triumph over others, but in unconditional inner peace.

**The origin of matter: Rome and Mor**
If we consider the German word *Rom* and reverse it, we get *Mor* . This deep root syllable is connected in many ancient languages to the origin, to the primal, maternal (Mutter, Mother, Mère). The external structure of Rome dissolves within, leading us back to the nurturing womb of creation.

**Finding one's own voice: Rome, Romë and Emor**
Both the French spelling *Rome* and the Albanian *Romë* , when phonetically reversed, produce *emor* (or ëmor). Here, a window opens into a completely different linguistic world: Hebrew. *Emor* translates as "Speak!" or "Say!"—it represents the spoken word, the creative Logos. While external Rome stands for silent laws carved in stone, the path inward ( *Emor* ) leads us to speak our own creative truth. 

**The dawning of inner light: Rómi and Ímar**
The pattern becomes particularly poetic in Greek. The word *Rhōmē* (Rómi) originally meant literally physical power, might, or strength. If we reverse the sound *of Rómi* , we arrive at *i-mó-r* . This sounds similar to the ancient Greek word *ἦμαρ* (ímar), which in Homer's writings means "day" or "daylight." When we release the urge for control and power within us, day breaks. The darkness of external struggle gives way to the light of awareness.

**The hidden path**
Thus, hidden within the name of a single city that once ruled the world is a complete blueprint for the human soul. The reverse teaches us: Find your own creative voice ( *Emor* ), return to the origin ( *Mor* ), let the light of knowledge dawn within you ( *Ímar* ), and you will experience profound peace ( *Mir* ) and all-encompassing love ( *Amor* / *Amorre* ). 

All roads lead to Rome – but only when we turn around on this road and look within ourselves do we truly arrive.
