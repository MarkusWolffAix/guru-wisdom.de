# The light in the story of Abraham (Ibrahim)
## Enlightenment on the spiritual path

The symbolism of light is beautiful and profound in the traditions about Abraham (Ibrahim) – especially when one considers that he came from a world that worshipped many nature deities.

Here is how the “light” is used in its story as a powerful symbol for spiritual awakening and realization:

## 1. The Search for the True Light (The Celestial Bodies)
One of the most famous and poetic narratives (which is described very vividly in the Quran in Surah 6, *Al-An'am* ) depicts Abraham's inner search for the true God using light sources.

* **The Star:** When night fell and he saw a bright star, he first said, "That is my lord!" But when the star set, he realized, *"I do not love those who set."*
* **The Moon:** Then he saw the moon rise in its bright light and thought it was larger and more permanent. But the moon, too, disappeared in the morning.
* **The sun:** Finally, the radiant, all-encompassing sun rose. Abraham thought: "This must be my Lord, this is the greatest!" But when the mighty sun also sank below the horizon in the evening, he drew his great conclusion.
* **The realization:** Abraham realized that no physical light—no matter how brightly it shines—is eternal or divine, as it is subject to the laws of rising and setting (i.e., creation). He turned instead to the invisible *Creator* of all these lights. For him, true light was no longer physical, but the spiritual realization of the One.

## 2. The Light in the Urfa Cave
If we go back to Urfa, light also plays a role in the local legends surrounding his birth. 

* According to tradition, Abraham's mother had to give birth to him in a dark cave and hide him there to protect him from King Nimrod.
* It is said that the cave did not remain dark, however. A miraculous light is said to have emanated from the newborn or illuminated the cave – a symbol that he was chosen to dispel the darkness of idolatry in his time.

## 3. Nimrod's fire becomes light and life
The story of how Nimrod threw Abraham into the fire is another powerful metaphor.

* Nimrod wanted to destroy Abraham with the most destructive form of light and heat (fire). 
* But God transformed the destructive power of fire into something cool, peaceful, and alive (water and fish). The fire lost its burning heat and instead became a protective "light" for Abraham.

## 4. Abraham as a “light-bringer” for the nations
In later Jewish and Christian traditions, Abraham's faith is often described as a beacon in a dark world. His unwavering commitment to justice, hospitality, and monotheistic belief made him a role model. Wisdom texts often state, in essence, that he carried the light of true knowledge of God from Mesopotamia out into the world (to Canaan and Egypt).

---

**Conclusion:** In Abraham's story, light is no longer an object to be worshipped (like the sun or fire for his ancestors), but a symbol of inner enlightenment, true faith, and the protection that God grants him.
