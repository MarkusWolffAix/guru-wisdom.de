# The breath of the city
## Why the protection of the Beverau and the Donkey Trail goes beyond climate.

There are places in a city that one hardly notices as spectacular in everyday life, yet which, like silent organs, keep the entire organism alive. In Aachen's southeast, where the dense development of the neighborhoods gradually gives way to wide meadows, old streams, and shady forest edges, lies such a place: the Beverau with its donkey path. Those who walk this path not only leave the noise of the streets behind but enter a space for breathing freely, for encounters, and for a vibrant connection with the earth. But this very unassuming treasure is at the heart of a fundamental debate that extends far beyond mere urban planning.

### The invisible life insurance of the boiler

Aachen lies nestled in a valley basin – a geographical feature that gives the city its sheltered character, but also makes it vulnerable on hot summer nights. When the sun has heated the asphalt and stones of the city center for hours, the basin needs a cooling counterbalance to prevent overheating. The open meadows of the Beverau act like a natural air conditioner on these nights. Here, the air cools, sinks, and flows like a gentle, invisible river through the Beverbach valley into the Frankenberger Quarter, the eastern quarter, and into the heart of the city.

Measurements taken during summer nights vividly demonstrate the immense power of this silent service provided by nature: While temperatures in the densely built-up streets of the city center rarely dip below 21 degrees Celsius, the air along the banks of the Beverau stream cools to below 15 degrees. This corridor of fresh air is not a luxury for a few strollers, but a vital protective function for the elderly, children, the sick, and ultimately for everyone who lives in this city. At the same time, the unsealed soils and the floodplains around the Beverbach stream store valuable water that would otherwise rush unchecked into the lower-lying parts of the city during heavy rainfall.

### An intervention in the living structure

This finely tuned ecological balance is contrasted by plans that would forever alter the face of the landscape. The adjacent barracks are slated for massive expansion on nearly 28 hectares – an area almost equivalent to 50 football fields. Plans include enormous training halls, a driver training area, and extensive paved surfaces. What appears on paper as an abstract square meterage for military infrastructure translates in reality into the loss of a protected landscape, the fragmentation of biotopes, and the severing of one of the most vital lifelines for the city's climate.

The loss would also be profound for the local residents. The donkey path is far more than just a connecting path between residential areas, Nellessen Park, and the adjacent woods. It's an easy access point to nature, where children build huts, older people can reach green spaces without long walks, and neighbors strike up conversations as they pass by. When such a space is replaced by fences, concrete, and halls, a city loses not only green space, but also a piece of its soul and its social heart.

### The question of true security

The debate surrounding the expansion of the Beverau area reveals a clash of two contrasting concepts of protection and security. On the one hand, there is the drive to rapidly expand military infrastructure without lengthy approval processes, supported by new laws that prioritize defense construction projects over municipal and environmental concerns. On the other hand, there is a growing realization among the public, relevant authorities, and the city itself that a security that destroys the foundations of their own lives is inherently contradictory.

True security begins where the foundations of life – cool air in hot summers, protection from flooding, healthy soil, and vibrant communities – are preserved. When the city of Aachen itself describes this location as the least suitable possible site and offers to work together to find compatible alternatives, it demonstrates that this is not about blind refusal, but about striving for reason and moderation.

### The power of connection

Perhaps the deepest wisdom of this conflict lies in what has been awakened by the threat to the donkey trail in Aachen. Out of concern for a piece of home right on their doorstep, a vibrant movement of people quickly emerged, people who refuse to accept a feeling of powerlessness. Neighbors, families, scientists, and conservationists have begun to look, listen, and raise their voices.

The donkey trail reminds us that we do not live separately from the landscape that surrounds us. How we treat the meadows, streams, and paths on our doorstep reflects how we treat ourselves and future generations. Preserving such a place means protecting the city's gentle breathing – and thus the vitality within ourselves.
