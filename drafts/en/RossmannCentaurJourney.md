# The Centaur's Path
## The Transformation of a Company and Its Founder

The story of large companies is often told only in balance sheets, branch networks, and growth rates. But looking deeper, a profoundly human and often mythical journey is frequently revealed. The development of the Rossmann drugstore chain and the life of its founder, Dirk Rossmann, are one such example—a story of unbridled energy, existential crises, and the maturation into a deep, holistic sense of responsibility.

It all begins in 1972 with an act of liberation. 25-year-old Dirk Rossmann breaks free from the old structures of his parents' business and opens Germany's first self-service drugstore in Hanover. It is a time of upheaval; fixed prices for drugstore items are being abolished, and Rossmann seizes this historic moment with almost impetuous energy. On the first day, customers literally storm the doors. What followed was a period of rapid, almost frenzied growth that carried the company far beyond the borders of Germany. It was a phase of unbridled forward momentum.

But every pure performance principle that operates without pause eventually takes its toll. In the late 1990s, the sky darkened over the company. The rapid expansion brought the corporation to the brink of financial ruin. Almost simultaneously, the founder's body also collapsed: A severe heart attack forced the then-fifty-something to an abrupt halt. In this existential crisis, confronted with his own mortality and the potential loss of his life's work, a decisive transformation took place. Rossmann realized that life is more than the relentless pursuit of success. He learned to let go, to share responsibility, and to bring partners on board.

The company recovered, but the founder himself also changed. In his later years, his perspective broadened. The once driven entrepreneur has become an advocate for environmental protection and climate justice, deeply engaged with the future of humanity and using his platform to draw attention to global injustices.

This entire journey is most strikingly encapsulated in the company's symbol: the centaur logo. At first glance, it's a charming visual representation of the company name—the "horse" and the "man." But on a deeper level, a fascinating metaphor unfolds. The centaur from Greek mythology unites two worlds within itself. The horse's body represents the earthbound, untamed, primal force of nature—the very force that propelled the company forward so tempestuously in its early years. The human torso, on the other hand, symbolizes the alert, reflective mind, reason, and a higher vision—qualities that came to the fore after the crisis.

This symbol becomes even more remarkable when one considers Chiron, the wisest of all centaurs. Chiron was not a wild beast, but a scholar and, above all, a master healer who understood the power of herbs and plants. That a centaur should guard the doors of a drugstore—a place originally dedicated to precisely that art of healing, care, and health—seems like an almost poetic coincidence.

Thus, the Rossmann logo becomes a reflection of the duality we all carry within us: the constant balancing of our driving, instinctual forces with our thoughtful, visionary mind. It reminds us that true maturity often only arises when the impetuous urge is tamed by the wisdom of experience and placed in the service of a greater whole.
