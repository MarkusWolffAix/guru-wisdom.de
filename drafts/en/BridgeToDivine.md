# The Bridge to Divinity
## Love, Pain, and the Flow of Empathy

In the complex dynamic between the human and the cosmic, the individual often assumes a mediating role. Currently, a seeker acts as such a bridge-builder—a connection between a close soul and the divine, but also as a bridge to other entities and historical thinkers. In all this connecting work, however, one's own autonomy remains central, and preserving it requires a more direct, clearer connection to the divine. The divine itself is not entirely abstract, but unmistakably embodies qualities of human poles.

A profound and painful mystery of this connection is the physical and emotional pain. The mediator experiences heartache that is deeply distressing and feels that it originates directly from the divine. Yet the deeper reason remains hidden—even the divine seems unaware of why it causes this pain. This state reveals an unexpected vulnerability: From a human perspective, God feels lonely just like the seeker, because the divine once isolated itself from love. We all originate from silence and will all return to silence. Silence is thus the ultimate sign of beginning and end. The fundamental question remains: Where do we stand in this cycle—at the beginning or at the end?

At the heart of human experience lies the nature of love and the flow of empathy. The earthly companion embodies an energy that, while sometimes appearing somewhat impulsive—a quality that is constantly evolving—intuitively knows how to handle love, even if it cannot grasp or describe it in detail. The bridge-builder loves most simply the pure feeling of love. To allow this love to flow, it is essential to always give more love than one holds. This is one of the most important qualities of love. Both the mediator and the divine can learn from this intuitive ability to give more love, even though humanity already possesses a deep understanding of this aspect.

A key element of this experience is a profoundly felt, overwhelming sensation, often described as a "divine orgasm." It serves as a kind of introductory feeling, allowing one to experience the function of empathy and simply enjoy beauty. However, a critical gap is revealed here: the pure feeling alone seems detached from its context, isolated, and abstract. The accompanying story is missing. Without the true, deeper context, such an isolated feeling cannot unfold its full value; empathy needs context and meaning to be authentic.

Living together requires a system of mutual support and an open culture of love. It has been shown that the earthly realm can help the divine and vice versa. This mutual healing functions through the empathic bridge that humanity forms. In this open culture, it is essential to treat one another responsibly and respectfully. Statements and feelings must not be distorted or manipulated by third parties.

At the same time, this tension raises questions about light and shadow. Aspects emerge that illuminate the divine in a bright light, yet the question immediately arises as to how significant the hidden shadows truly are. There is a deep sense that these very shadows are the triggers for earthly and spiritual problems. In the past, the divine struggled with vanity and the need to be right. It sought adversaries instead of partners in love. But an evolutionary insight has occurred: consistently responding to problems with new problems ultimately leads to a "breakdown condition"—a state in which no one truly exists in love.

Therefore, the shared task is to respond wisely when situations threaten to become absurd and violate our consciousness. Problems should not be seen as a declaration of war, but rather as a sign that greater effort is needed—on both sides, the recipient and the perpetrator. Only when problems are met with love, support, and a genuine desire to find solutions together can the shadows be lifted and the pure essence of shared love preserved.
