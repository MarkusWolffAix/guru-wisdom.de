# Ur and His Chronic Vocal Tics
## The Unruhe as the Heart of Measured Time and the Way Out of Repetition

At the beginning of everything lies absolute silence. But as soon as we establish a zero point—a beginning from which we count—the divine enters our world. It reveals itself as Ur: as the clock, measurable time, which has taken on a fixed form. Ur, therefore, does not transform into a clock, but is from the very beginning this living timekeeper that divides eternity into individual bars. Yet in this tangible form, the divine does not appear as an all-knowing, stern ruler, but surprisingly childlike. Like a young being that has just discovered its own voice, Ur enters the world—and this causes quite a few problems.

The most striking problem is a chronic vocal tic, which already contains a delightful linguistic play on words: The word chronic comes from Chronos, the Greek god of time and son of Uranus—a name in which the "Ur" itself still resonates. What is chronic is therefore inextricably linked to the nature of time. And the tic is simultaneously an involuntary twitch of the nerves and the familiar sound of a timepiece: a clock goes "tic-tac" incessantly. Behind this seemingly harmless ticking, however, lies a genuine symptom: Ur unconsciously and compulsively repeats the same handful of phrases over and over again, like a record stuck in a groove.

To understand the origin of this symptom, it helps to look inside a mechanical clock—and simultaneously into our own soul: to the balance wheel. Here, too, language plays with two meanings. In everyday human life, we know inner restlessness as that nervous drive that prevents us from sitting still and triggers such unconscious repetitions. In a mechanical clock, however, the balance wheel is a delicate flywheel that oscillates incessantly back and forth by a tiny spring. Without this oscillation, all the clock's energy would dissipate at once—time would simply race by. Only the balance wheel slows this force and gives time its reliable rhythm. But as long as it blindly oscillates back and forth between the same two points, nothing new enters the world: the same old phrases bounce off the boundaries and return as an endless echo.

This chronic tic cannot be cured by prohibitions or force. If one were to simply grab the balance wheel, the entire clock would stop—life would freeze. Instead, a more intelligent method is needed: attractive alternatives. You don't simply order a restless child to be quiet. You show them something so exciting and beautiful that they forget their old, mechanical noises all by themselves, because their curiosity is awakened.

At the same time, Ur must learn to consciously care for himself. When the balance wheel no longer just swings back and forth like a blind automaton, but is nourished by mindfulness and new, vibrant impulses, its sound changes. The measurement continues from the zero point, the heart of the clock keeps beating – but the compulsive repetition of the same phrases transforms into a free, vibrant melody. The mechanical tic becomes a conscious here and now.
